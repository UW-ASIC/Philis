Constructive Placement and Routing for Common-Centroid Capacitor Arrays in Binary-Weighted and Split DACs

Nibedita Karmokar, Arvind K. Sharma, Jitesh Poojary, Meghna Madhusudan, Ramesh Harjani and Sachin S. Sapatnekar · IEEE TCAD 42(9), 2023, pp. 2782–2795

Spiral/chessboard construction, terminal routing, bridge geometry and matching/settling tradeoffs.

Open supplied PDF · 14 physical pages · File identity and coverage

This paper supplies a constructive array generator whose purpose is an electrical tradeoff: retain adequate matching while reducing the vias and wire resistance that limit capacitor-array settling. Its spiral and block-chessboard patterns are useful candidates for Philis, not a universal best arrangement.

How to use this paper in the handbook

Read DAC arrays for the full construction procedure and worked examples, capacitor devices for unit geometry, and gradient matching for the statistical assumptions. This source page records exactly what the supplied 2023 paper supports.

Problem, assumptions, and modeled circuit

The authors target binary-weighted and split charge-scaling DACs, especially a commercial 12 nm process with high wire/via resistance and directional routing. The unit count of a conventional N-bit bank with its grounded dummy is 2N; a split topology uses two smaller banks and an attenuation capacitor. That architecture reduces unit count but introduces a particularly sensitive bridge element and separate top-plate nodes. Layout generation must preserve the supplied architecture. §I–II, PDF pp. 1–3 §V, commercial 12 nm evaluation setup, PDF p. 10

The model separates intended capacitor values, top-to-bottom parasitics, top-to-substrate parasitics, and bottom-plate loading. The first two can perturb effective ratios; shunt loading and interconnect resistance influence power and settling. Coupling depends on excitation: two terminals at equal potential exchange no incremental charge through their mutual capacitor in that excitation, but this does not mean the physical coupling vanishes under every transition. §II, PDF pp. 2–3

Important implementation consequence. Preserve terminal identities in extraction. A single total-C scalar cannot reproduce these distinctions or calculate code-dependent output error.

The three pattern families and their reasons

FamilyConstructionReason and cost
SpiralPlace increasing capacitor multiplicities outward, placing each unit together with its centrally reflected partner.Adjacent units of a bit can connect with few bends/vias. Reduced dispersion may worsen variation-induced INL/DNL.
ChessboardDistribute the largest bit across one color, then recursively fill remaining positions.High dispersion samples a common spatial field, but disconnected components require substantial routing and many vias.
Block chessboardUse a fine chessboard for lower-count capacitors and larger blocks for outer, larger capacitors; vary the block size.Trades dispersion against the RC cost of the critical bits. It creates a family of compromises rather than one winning pattern.

§IV-A, PDF pp. 7–8

The unit-count exception is significant: C₀ and C₁ each have one unit in the binary bank, so they cannot both individually occupy the same centroid in a nonoverlapping grid. The paper places them near the center. Call this an approximation for those members, not an exact all-member CC certificate. Split banks have analogous odd-count and bridge-placement exceptions. Odd-count exception, PDF p. 7 Split placement, PDF p. 9

Routing is part of the construction

Neighboring units of a capacitor become connected components found by breadth-first search. Bottom-plate branch wires connect units, vertical trunks connect components, and peripheral bridges join trunks. Each symmetric connection is mirrored. Separate top-plate routing uses a graph/MST to connect electrically common plates while minimizing added top-to-substrate capacitance. In a split DAC the LSB and MSB top-plate graphs remain separate except for the attenuation capacitor. §IV-B–C, PDF pp. 8–10

The authors use parallel wires to reduce resistance on critical bits while respecting discrete wire widths. A widened or parallel-routed critical bit can cause a different bit to become the slowest; therefore repeat the all-bit delay evaluation after every improvement. More parallel routes eventually add enough capacitance and channel demand to give diminishing returns. Results and Fig. 15, PDF pp. 11–12

Philis adaptation. Give capacitor-array macros internal connectivity, route-resource demand, and terminal-specific extracted RC. The current Capacitor::draw constructs each member as a merged plate or comb; that representation is not yet a routed common-centroid unit array. A dedicated generator should coexist with the simple primitive instead of relabeling it.

Metrics and equations that need careful interpretation

Equations (17)–(18) define code-step DNL and ideal-transfer INL in LSB units. Preserve their reference convention: endpoint-fitted or best-fit INL removes different errors. For an N-bit transfer with codes 0…2N−1 there are 2N−1 adjacent transitions. Report the worst code and transition, not just an array-level average. §III, PDF p. 4

Paper's frequency metric, Eq. (32):
f = 1 / [2 (N+2) ln(2) τ]
τ is the worst relevant RC charging delay over the bits.

The authors label this a 3-dB frequency, but its N-dependent expression is tied to their charge/discharge settling model. It is not interchangeable with the sinusoidal −3 dB pole of a first-order RC, 1/(2πτ). Philis should name the reported metric explicitly and validate transient settling to a stated LSB error. This distinction is our interpretation of the displayed equation, visually checked in the PDF. §III-C and Eq. (32), PDF pp. 5–6

For the nonunit MOM bridge capacitor, the paper derives an effective perimeter/area-like condition. With coupling length l and height H, its small edge-shift model gives ΔC/C≈2δ(1/H+1/l). Matching this quantity to the unit capacitor while solving for target capacitance gives two geometric equations. The result assumes the paper's MOM construction, small common edge shifts, and continuous dimensions; legal pitch quantization and fringe effects require extraction afterward. §III-D, PDF p. 6

What the results establish—and what remains uncertain

The reported experiments show a real tradeoff: spiral layouts favor the modeled frequency, chessboard layouts favor dispersion/linearity, and block chessboards occupy intermediate points. Table II's 8-bit examples, for instance, give about 1411 MHz for spiral and 339 MHz for block chessboard under the paper's frequency model, with different INL/DNL. These values are evidence for the mechanism in that setup, not Philis performance targets. Tables I–II, PDF p. 11

The paper itself warns that its commonly reused gradient and correlation parameters are inherited from earlier work and are not a measured capacitor correlation characterization. This is an especially valuable methodological result: do not turn ρ=0.9, a 1 mm correlation length, or a 10 ppm gradient into default manufacturing truth. Sweep uncertain values and label them assumed until the PDK or measurements justify them. Parameter discussion, PDF p. 12

Its analytical use of 3σ shifts does not automatically give the joint worst-case yield of all codes and parasitics. The sums share random variables; separately pushing numerator and denominator by the same sign of 3σ is not a general covariance-aware worst case. Use joint samples or sensitivity/covariance analysis for a yield claim. The nominal linearity analysis also assumes an ideal output amplifier, so switch resistance, finite buffer gain, settling, reference impedance, and dynamic error must be added for a complete converter test.

Reading coverage and limits

Read all 14 PDF pages, including both DAC circuit models, systematic/correlated variation equations, nonunit bridge derivation, placement algorithm, both routing procedures, parameter limitations, results, references, and author material. Visually checked PDF p. 5 for Eq. (32) and PDF p. 11 for the dense tables and routing comparison. The handbook's generator architecture and joint-yield tests are proposed Philis extensions; the paper's experimental improvement ratios have not been reproduced here.
