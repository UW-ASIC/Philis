Layout-aware sizing and retargeting

Connect authorized sizing, module variants, parasitics, compaction and process migration.

Device sizing and layout must negotiate a realizable circuit

Sizing chooses an electrical design, while placement and routing create the geometry that changes its behavior. Treating those activities as completely independent creates a predictable failure: the nominally optimized schematic has no feasible low-parasitic physical implementation, or the layout's smallest cell variants consume the margin that sizing assumed remained available. Layout-aware sizing closes this loop by evaluating meaningful physical alternatives during electrical optimization.

Lampaert discusses device generation, simulation-model synchronization and the limitations of a separated placement/routing flow. The layout-aware sizing chapter in the topological volume combines fixed topology, geometric constraints, layout evaluation and parasitic models inside a simulation loop. Graeb supplies the multiobjective and robust optimization formulation. Together they support an iterative architecture, not the claim that minimizing transistor area automatically minimizes a useful analog layout. Lampaert, PDF pp. 66–82, pp. 167–172; Topological Approaches, PDF pp. 259–275; Design Centering, PDF pp. 75–83.

Separate electrical variables from implementation variables

Electrical variables include total device width and length, multiplicity conventions, resistor or capacitor values, bias current and allowed device flavors. Physical variables include unit count, row/column arrangement, permitted finger realization, diffusion sharing, orientation, contacts, terminal access, placement topology, wire layers and widths. Some physical choices preserve the intended ideal netlist; others change the circuit or its model parameters and require an updated electrical reference.

For a hypothetical legal refolding operation, total width may satisfy Wtotal = Nf × Wfinger with unchanged L. This identity alone does not prove electrical equivalence. Diffusion area and perimeter, gate resistance, contact distribution, source/drain assignment, local environment and model interpretation can change. A simulator's multiplicity parameter may not have the same statistical meaning as independent physical fingers. State the intended equivalence explicitly, then verify generated connectivity and extracted device parameters.

Philis already preserves an important boundary. In Mosfet::enumerate (line 26), the generator keeps schematic finger count and per-finger width; the comments explain that an arbitrary refold would change the drawn device and LVS reference. It also withholds the ABAB interdigitation option because the current shared-diffusion construction would short drains at inter-device boundaries. These are physical/electrical constraints on the available variant space, not missing aesthetic patterns.

Proposed action: classify variants into electrically equivalent implementations and intentional sizing changes. The first class can be searched within a fixed electrical design; the second invokes a sizing transaction that updates the schematic reference, unitization, statistical interpretation and parasitic model. This avoids using a convenient rectangle reshape to silently change the circuit.

Why a larger device or smaller cell can be the wrong choice

Larger matched area can reduce a calibrated random mismatch term approximately as σ ∝ 1/√(WL). It also increases intrinsic capacitance and physical footprint. Derived scaling example: under an ideal long-channel strong-inversion square-law model in saturation at fixed drain current, gm ≈ √(2μCox(W/L)ID), whereas a gate-capacitance component scales approximately with WL. Combining those scalings at fixed L gives gm/C ∝ 1/√W when that capacitance dominates. This algebra assumes the same mobility and oxide capacitance, neglects short-channel effects, and does not apply across a change of inversion regime. The capacitance scaling is an ideal gate-oxide area approximation at a comparable bias, ignoring overlap, fringe and depletion; actual Cgs and Cgd depend on bias. The model is illustrative and not a replacement for the PDK. Its source foundations are Hastings §12.1, Eqs. (12.3)–(12.4), PDF p. 576, §12.1.1, Eqs. (12.6) and (12.9), PDF p. 577, and the ideal planar capacitance relation in §7.1, Eq. (7.2), PDF p. 327; the gm/C combination above is our derivation.

More fingers can reduce a long gate's resistance and change diffusion sharing, but add gate straps, contacts, local interconnect and access congestion. A compact common-centroid pattern can shorten some connections and lengthen others. A dummy or well extension increases bounding area while preserving a valuable local environment. Evaluate each implementation with the physical effects it changes, rather than assigning a universal cost to finger count or dummy count. Lampaert, PDF pp. 66–74.

A generated cell should therefore expose more than width and height: terminal shapes and access alternatives, unit membership, electrical mapping, occupied and reserved regions, diffusion geometry, relevant device-environment parameters, internal connectivity and model revision. A fast interface-only generator may support early search, followed by full geometry for selected variants; their agreement must be checked because the fast view guides the optimization. PDF pp. 73–74.

Worked example: local area dominance can fail at the parent level

Suppose two electrically equivalent cell templates have the same device and external load capacitance of 50 fF and drive through an illustrative 20 kΩ output resistance. Template A occupies 12×6 µm² = 72 µm², but its output access forces 95 fF of routed capacitance. Template B occupies 14×6 µm² = 84 µm² and exposes the output near its destination, producing 40 fF of routed capacitance. In a one-pole screening model fp = 1/(2πRoutCtotal), A gives about 54.9 MHz and B about 88.4 MHz. If the requirement is at least 70 MHz, only B passes this model.

Pruning B solely because A is narrower and equal-height loses the only feasible parent-level result in this example. Shape-curve dominance is safe only with respect to the attributes and parent contexts represented in the comparison. The hierarchical chapter retains width/height alternatives and uses additional criteria, but a modern electrical interface may need terminal-access and parasitic classes as well. Topological Approaches, PDF pp. 137–147.

Action: retain a bounded Pareto set of implementation variants across footprint, sensitive-pin access, internal parasitics and required environment. A variant should be removed only when another is no worse for the relevant context or when the loss of search coverage is explicitly accepted. This keeps enumeration tractable without treating local geometry as the complete circuit objective.

Nested and joint optimization make different cost/coverage tradeoffs

A nested flow chooses an electrical sizing vector, generates legal physical variants, runs placement and routing, extracts parasitics and evaluates specifications. The outer optimizer changes sizing using those results. This cleanly separates tools but makes every outer evaluation expensive. A joint flow changes sizing and layout variables together, potentially discovering interactions sooner, but faces a larger mixed discrete/continuous search and more frequent model invalidation.

A practical hybrid begins with a fixed electrical design and a small set of validated templates, improves their physical implementation, then allows controlled sizing changes when no physical alternative supplies enough margin. Local sensitivities suggest which sizes are worth changing. Regenerate geometry and re-extract after changes, because a new size can cross a contact-count, row-count or routing-topology boundary. A smooth surrogate through such a discontinuity must not be treated as exact.

Warm-starting from the previous layout reduces cost, but can bias every new sizing toward the same local topology. Occasionally compare alternative initializations or templates, especially after large sizing changes. A stochastic inner placer also makes the outer objective noisy: distinguish a genuinely better sizing from one lucky inner seed. Use controlled seeds for comparisons and repeated evaluations where differences are comparable to search variability.

Templates and parasitic models need a declared domain

A template preserves known-good relationships: transistor grouping, symmetry, routing topology, local environment and critical connections. It can make sizing evaluation fast and predictable. The price is limited coverage. A topology valid for one width ratio may become congested or geometrically impossible for another. Independently preserving horizontal and vertical relations may still allow overlap after resizing unless the combined constraints guarantee separation throughout the supported dimension range. Topological Approaches, PDF pp. 196–203.

Use a template contract: legal device-size ranges, allowed ratios, orientation and unitization choices, terminal-access constraints, complete separation relations and model validity conditions. Test boundary sizes and aspect ratios, not only the nominal example. If a parameter leaves the domain, regenerate using another template or report that the current template cannot realize it.

The layout-aware sizing chapter contrasts direct extraction with analytical device-parasitic estimates and lookup models of routing. A table with m samples on each of n dimensions grows as mⁿ, so blindly sampling every design parameter becomes infeasible. First identify sensitive parasitic/output relationships, then model their relevant dimensions, retaining independent validation points. A model fitted to one topology and PDK must not be reused after a different routing structure or device environment appears. PDF pp. 272–275.

Action: maintain a staged model hierarchy. Early search uses cheap footprint and access estimates; shortlisted candidates get route-aware RC estimates; finalist sizing/layout pairs receive full extraction and circuit analysis. Record prediction error against each higher-fidelity stage. Increase detail where error can change feasibility or ranking, rather than spending equal computation on every insensitive net.

Process retargeting preserves intent, not dimensions

A source layout contains valuable design decisions: matched groups, signal flow, critical pin placement, shielding topology, well relationships and environment equivalence. A new process changes spacing, contact structures, layer resistances, device models and sometimes the valid device architecture. Uniform geometric scaling therefore does not establish electrical or manufacturing equivalence. Retargeting must preserve the intended relationships while regenerating process-specific implementation choices. Topological Approaches, PDF pp. 215–221.

The retargeting chapter develops generalized graph formulations for linear constraints and discusses contact-array reconstruction, conditional rules, changes in orthogonal ordering and layout-dependent effects. These details explain why simple compaction is insufficient. A narrower target wire may require more vias for current reliability; a compacted well edge may alter device behavior; the ordering assumptions used in the next compaction direction can become stale after the first. PDF pp. 238–247.

Proposed sequence: recover semantic electrical/physical intent from the source; map device and layer roles to the target process; generate target-compatible device variants; solve target constraints; reroute critical connections; extract and re-evaluate the target circuit. Keep source-layout evidence as provenance, not as a target-process certificate. A topology can be a useful initialization while still requiring new sizing and robustness analysis.

When preserving surroundings, identify the physical mechanism. Copying a numerical well margin from one process can be meaningless in another; preserving equivalent response requires the target PDK's supported layout-dependent-effect model or a calibrated conservative rule. If that model is unavailable, report the inherited heuristic and its limitation rather than claiming electrical equivalence.

Map the loop onto the existing Philis interfaces

Cell (line 39) already separates deterministic variant enumeration from drawing. sizing (line 148) obtains per-member finger counts and unit dimensions from unitization. VariantSpace (line 20) stores pre-drawn alternatives; choose_variants (line 311) selects the actual macros; try_reshape (line 460) changes a candidate's variant and pin offsets. These are concrete extension points for richer variant descriptors and electrical equivalence metadata.

Actual area must include required surrounding structures and routing reservations. guard_rings (line 18) constructs rings after placement using reserved halos. Any sizing objective that counts only active transistor WL misses this area and its placement interactions. Track drawn cell area, reserved footprint and final routed block area separately, then optimize the measure relevant to the floorplan contract.

Before allowing sizing as an automated move, create an explicit transaction that regenerates unitization, all affected cell variants, electrical reference data, parasitic baselines, statistical models, current estimates and dependent constraints. The reason is consistency: a wider input device changes more than its rectangle. Apply the provenance and invalidation model in constraint systems so stale analysis cannot certify the new design.

Evidence needed before trusting layout-aware sizing

Check every generated variant for intended connectivity and extracted device parameters across its declared size domain. Compare equivalent variants using the same ideal circuit reference, then quantify their parasitic differences. Verify that illegal sharing patterns are excluded by generator preconditions, not merely penalized after drawing. Test changes at discrete thresholds: a new finger, contact row, dummy choice or routing layer can reveal failures invisible to small continuous perturbations.

For the optimization loop, compare three baselines on the same circuit and process: nominal sizing followed by layout; sizing with estimated parasitics; and sizing with validated layout feedback. Report final extracted specifications, robustness estimates, area, runtime and unresolved analyses. The historical source examples motivate the comparison but do not prove that a particular surrogate or template will help this engine.

Keep a Pareto archive of verified sizing/layout pairs and their model context. A single weighted scalar can hide a meaningful area-versus-matching or speed-versus-power tradeoff; the archive makes those choices reviewable. Completion means the delivered physical realization satisfies its selected electrical and manufacturing contract with stated evidence, not that the outer sizing optimizer stopped changing W and L.
