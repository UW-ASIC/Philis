A constraint-driven handbook for automated P&R

The optimal analog layout

A teaching handbook for producing better analog layouts through justified constraints: what to preserve, why it matters physically, how to generate it, where techniques fail, and how to verify the realized circuit.

30 topic chapters · 84 constraint entries · 13 source companions · 56 original SVG diagrams · approximately 129,000 words

The governing principle: an optimal layout is defined relative to a circuit contract, process models, operating and variation scenarios, and chosen objectives. Geometry is a means of controlling electrical behavior and manufacturability. The engine must distinguish required feasibility, limited physical proxies, and preferences among feasible alternatives.

Before using a theory claim in a code comment: consult the precise provenance records for its source location, assumptions and attribution type. Read the accuracy audit for corrections and unresolved reference gaps. This handbook is audited engineering guidance, not a certification of universal correctness or exhaustive process coverage.

How to use the handbook

  1. Establish the target. Begin with optimality, circuit intent and constraint architecture. These define what the engine must preserve and what a pass means.
  2. Learn the mechanisms. Follow the device, matching, routing and reliability chapters below. Each connects actions to physical reasons, equations or models, techniques, tradeoffs and acceptance checks.
  3. Turn knowledge into rules. Use the 84-entry constraint catalog as the implementation index. Requirements are conditional on device family, PDK, scenario and the intended circuit metric.
  4. Close the loop. Study electrical feedback, yield, coverage and validation, then use the code-grounded roadmap to prioritize work.

For evidence or deeper source-specific detail, follow page citations and the source companions. These are supporting references; the main handbook is organized by the engine's design problems.

How to interpret the recommendations

Source findings explain what the supplied research supports. Derived examples teach a mechanism under stated assumptions. Code observations describe the reviewed workspace. Proposed constraints and experiments describe improvements to implement and validate. A textbook technique becomes a production rule only with the required process data, circuit scope and verification evidence.
