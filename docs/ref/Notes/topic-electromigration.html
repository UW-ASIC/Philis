Electromigration and interconnect lifetime

Use qualified current, temperature, length and via models rather than one universal width rule.

The weakest current-carrying feature determines the route

An optimal analog route carries every required waveform for the intended lifetime while keeping voltage drop, capacitance, coupling, area, and congestion within budget. Reliability belongs to the complete conductive path: device contacts, pin access, metal segments, bends, necks, vias, and package interfaces. A generous wire width does not compensate for a narrow pin or one overloaded cut. The governing source is Lienig and Thiele’s Fundamentals of Electromigration-Aware Integrated Circuit Design; this chapter translates its mechanisms into a routing method and states where process characterization is indispensable. Current-aware physical-design flow.

1. Electromigration is material transport with local consequences

Current transfers momentum to atoms in a metal. Atomic motion follows available bulk, grain-boundary, and interface paths. Damage is associated with divergence of this material flux: atoms leave one region faster than they arrive, creating a void, or accumulate elsewhere, producing an extrusion. Interfaces, contacts, corners, temperature gradients, and mechanical boundaries make the flux nonuniform.

The electrical failure criterion can be an open circuit, short circuit, or a smaller resistance increase that already violates analog accuracy. A degrading source connection shifts a transistor’s operating point; a degrading reference branch creates gain error; a degrading supply path reduces output swing. Reliability should therefore use the product’s allowed electrical degradation rather than assume that only a complete open counts as failure. As a void reduces area, current density and Joule heating increase, which can accelerate further transport. Mechanisms and quantification, PDF 25–33.

Temperature gradients drive thermomigration, and stress gradients drive stress migration. These effects interact with EM. Back stress created by atom accumulation can oppose EM in a confined conductor; stress from thermal expansion, processing, or packaging is a different contribution. Aluminum grain-boundary behavior, copper interface behavior, cap adhesion, liner properties, and dielectric stiffness explain why the same geometric shape can have different reliability in different stacks. Diffusion paths; Thermal and mechanical coupling.

2. Average density is a screening metric, not a local field solution

javg = I/(wt), while local current density follows J = −σ∇φ.

Width w and conducting thickness t must refer to the actual cross-section represented by the reliability model. Etch loss, barrier occupancy, thickness variation, and current crowding can make drawn area an optimistic substitute. A PDK rule stated as current per width, such as mA/µm, often incorporates thickness and process assumptions; a cross-sectional current-density rule in A/cm² does not. The interface should name the quantity and its units instead of storing an untyped number called “EM limit.”

Illustrative conversion. A 1 mA current in a 1 µm-wide, 0.5 µm-thick conducting section has 2 mA/µm² average density, or 2×105 A/cm². The same current per width in a layer with twice the conducting thickness has half this average density. These are arithmetic examples, not safe operating limits.

A field or resistance-mesh solution reveals where current crowds in bends and landings. A mathematically sharp corner may have a singular local field; evaluating that singular point as manufactured reality is inappropriate, but smoothing every troublesome corner is also inappropriate. Use process-representative geometry, a mesh-convergence study, and a characterized spatial criterion. The source’s pin-access discussion is particularly relevant: connecting to different parts of the same legal pin can create different internal bottlenecks. Wire, via, and pin verification, PDF 99–105.

3. Derive branch currents before choosing widths

For a tree route, select an edge orientation and cut the edge. The current through it is the algebraic sum of currents on either side. This includes interconnect shunt/displacement currents when they matter: represent them as loads in the extracted tree, or state the approximation that they are negligible. Summing only device-terminal currents is not exact for an arbitrarily capacitive transient interconnect. Compute this sum for each operating mode and time point before taking a maximum, mean, or RMS value. Current directions may reverse during charging, discharging, startup, or signal swing. A maximum absolute scalar attached to the net loses the information needed to reconstruct those events.

Ie(t) = Σ Iterminal(t); Iavg = (1/τ)∫Ie(t)dt; Irms = √[(1/τ)∫Ie²(t)dt].

Two correlated pulses add; two opposite pulses can cancel on a shared branch while each local branch remains heavily loaded. Their separate RMS values do not reveal which case applies. Signed interval bounds can give conservative branch limits when waveforms are unavailable, but independent interval extrema can describe combinations that cannot occur. Preserve that conservatism and its reason. For meshes, current division depends on extracted resistance and boundary sources, so solve the network rather than assigning current by geometric distance. Terminal and branch-current modeling, PDF 83–90.

Topology and ampacity influence one another. A shortest-length tree may create a long heavy-current trunk; a slightly longer topology can reduce total width×length and parasitic loading by branching earlier or using a suitable upper layer. Reattaching a load invalidates currents on affected branches. The router should optimize a current-annotated graph, with cost and legality recomputed after topology changes.

4. DC, RMS, and peak currents answer different questions

Mean directional current is related to directional transport under an appropriate recovery model. RMS current determines average I²R heating when resistance is approximately fixed. Peak current and pulse duration constrain separate transient damage mechanisms. Current reversal can partially relieve EM, but does not eliminate heating; zero signed average is not a reliability exemption. Low-frequency reversal, unequal pulses, and changing temperature can behave differently from a high-frequency symmetric waveform. The book recommends simultaneous consideration of relevant current classes, while its numerical frequency thresholds remain examples rather than universal boundaries. Current classification; Frequency effects.

Worked example with invented capabilities. A branch carries 10 mA for 10% of each period and zero otherwise. Its average, RMS, and peak currents are 1, 3.16, and 10 mA. Suppose a hypothetical characterized deck at the chosen temperature allows 1 mA average per µm, 2 mA RMS per µm, and 5 mA peak per µm for this pulse duration. The corresponding widths are 1, 1.58, and 2 µm. The starting requirement is the maximum, 2 µm, followed by manufacturing rules and any required geometry correction.

Suppose the same hypothetical via allows 0.8 mA average, 1.2 mA RMS, and 4 mA peak. Ideal equal sharing gives a minimum of max[ceil(1/0.8), ceil(3.16/1.2), ceil(10/4)] = 3 cuts. If a validated template has a maximum-cut/mean-cut crowding factor of 1.5, the preliminary requirement becomes four cuts. That factor must be reevaluated for the actual four-cut geometry because adding cuts changes current sharing. This example explains the calculation; none of its capacities is a recommendation for a real process.

5. Temperature and lifetime cannot be hidden inside one permanent constant

t50 = Cj−nexp(Ea/kT).

At fixed lifetime: jallow(T)/jallow(Tref) = exp[(Ea/nk)(1/T − 1/Tref)].

This generalized Black relation describes a calibrated median failure time. Its current exponent, activation energy, coefficient, geometry, failure mechanism, and temperature range are part of the model. The median is not the low-failure-probability lifetime of a product containing many segments. A reliability target needs the distribution, confidence, allowed electrical degradation, and system allocation in addition to a nominal fitted relation. Black relation; Mission profile and statistical reliability.

Illustrative temperature calculation. With Ea = 0.8 eV, n = 2, and unchanged current density, raising temperature from 373 K to 398 K changes the modeled median lifetime by exp[(0.8/k)(1/398 − 1/373)] ≈ 0.209. Maintaining the same modeled lifetime would reduce allowable density to about √0.209 = 0.458 of its former value. These parameters are illustrative. The large sensitivity demonstrates why an ambient-temperature assumption can be inadequate near a heater or a self-heating metal neck.

A mission profile describes time spent in relevant operating states, temperatures, currents, storage conditions, and startup or transient events. Damage cannot generally be assessed at arithmetic-average current and temperature because the model is nonlinear and their correlation matters. A hot high-current mode can dominate. If the reliability methodology permits linear cumulative damage, a sum such as ΣΔt/tlife(mode) can be used as its approximation; Black’s relation alone does not establish that accumulation law, sequence independence, or recovery behavior. Use the foundry-approved model and state the assumptions.

The supplied book’s PDF page 101 prints a temperature factor that decreases above the reference temperature while multiplying required via count by that factor. Under the fixed-lifetime relation above, a decreasing allowable-current factor must instead reduce per-via capacity, or its inverse must multiply count. The page was visually checked. Resolve this apparent convention/sign inconsistency against the qualified model before implementation; enforce that hotter operation cannot require fewer cuts under otherwise unchanged positive-activation-energy assumptions. Printed equations 3.25–3.26.

6. Via arrays must share current electrically

Current chooses the full resistive network formed by lower metal, each cut, and upper metal. Inner cuts at a turn can have shorter access paths and carry disproportionately large current. The sum of nominal single-cut capacities is valid only when the array’s characterized current distribution supports it. Via count also serves a different purpose: redundant connections improve tolerance to missing cuts, overlay errors, incomplete fill, or other manufacturing faults. Manufacturing redundancy and EM margin should be reported separately. Via defects, sharing, and geometry, PDF 134–140.

Generate legal templates for different entry/exit directions and widths, then characterize their maximum local utilization across temperature and process variation. Include the contact or pin feeding the array. Increasing array footprint can improve metal area while worsening path inequality or consuming routing channels. The selected candidate should satisfy the worst cut and surrounding-metal requirements, not merely a target cut count.

7. The Blech effect is a boundary-condition result

When atom transport is blocked at appropriate boundaries, accumulated stress can create a force opposing the electrical driving force. In a simplified one-dimensional confined conductor, balancing these forces gives a critical product with magnitude

|jL|crit = ΩΔσ/(e|Z*|ρ).

Ω is atomic volume, Δσ the allowable stress difference, e the elementary charge, Z* the dimensionless effective charge number, and ρ resistivity. The length L belongs to the physical diffusion domain. It is not the distance between two arbitrary waypoints in the routing database. Adding a corner, segment split, or bookkeeping node does not introduce an atom-blocking boundary. Critical-length derivation and limitations, PDF 117–127.

Applying a short-segment exemption requires qualified material confinement, interface conditions, initial stress, electrical direction, temperature, and failure criterion. A branch junction can couple material reservoirs and stress between edges. A steady state that appears safe may still be reached through a damaging transient. A void that stops growing may still produce an unacceptable resistance increase. Consequently, “every edge has jL below a constant” is not a general reliability proof for a routed tree.

Via-above and via-below configurations in a dual-Damascene stack can have different permissible void volumes before losing contact. Reservoir metal can help supply atoms at one end and weaken useful back stress at another. Reversing current can reverse those roles. Bamboo microstructure can reduce grain-boundary transport while higher resistance, surface diffusion, or self-heating erases the benefit. These techniques should be enabled only as process-qualified routing capabilities, with all relevant boundaries attached to the rule. Bamboo structures; Via orientation and reservoirs.

8. Couple electrical and thermal evaluation

Resistance produces heat, temperature changes resistivity and diffusion, and a geometry repair changes both electrical and thermal behavior. A practical iteration solves currents and voltages, computes power, updates local temperature, updates resistance and reliability limits, and repeats until the chosen tolerances are met. The thermal environment includes neighboring devices, dielectric layers, substrate, package, and cooling boundary conditions. A silicon placement-temperature estimate cannot alone predict the temperature of a narrow upper-metal neck.

For fast search, use conservative calibrated pattern models or a reduced thermal network. For unusual junctions, high utilization, or strong self-heating, escalate model fidelity. The source discusses reusable routing elements specifically to avoid a full multiphysics solve for every candidate. Their composability needs checking: adjoining patterns may change current direction, reservoir boundaries, or the thermal environment. Simulation methods; Reliable routing-element libraries.

9. Data and algorithm the engine needs

InputWhy it is needed
Signed terminal waveforms or bounded scenarios, including startup and modesDerives branch demand without losing direction, simultaneity, or duty cycle.
Per-layer and per-via capabilities, thickness/resistance, geometry range, current metric, temperature, lifetime, confidence, and provenanceConverts current demand into a defensible physical requirement.
Pin/contact access models and legal routing templatesPrevents hidden bottlenecks at the device boundary.
Mission profile and electrothermal boundary modelConnects local usage with application lifetime and heating.
Qualified special-rule domainsPrevents unsupported Blech, recovery, reservoir, or material exemptions.
  1. Construct candidate topology and calculate branch-current scenarios.
  2. Choose layer, width, pin access, and via template from compatible capabilities.
  3. Extract resistance and evaluate voltage drop, current sharing, and local crowding.
  4. Compute temperature and update temperature-dependent electrical and reliability quantities.
  5. Repair limiting geometry while preserving manufacturability and analog constraints.
  6. Recompute affected branches and parasitics after every repair.
  7. Verify the final geometry with independent checks and report the limiting feature and model coverage.

The sequence is iterative because its variables are coupled. Width-only repair may be sufficient for an ordinary segment, while moving a pin landing, changing the layer transition, or changing topology can be a better response to a local bottleneck.

10. Philis integration and verification

The detailed router currently uses EM_UA_PER_UM = 1000 near line 38 and a scalar net_current_ua through em_width near line 105. This provides grid-rounded DC width sizing, but does not express branch-specific waveforms, temperature, per-layer capability, via sharing, or lifetime. Preserve its utility as a fast initial estimate while replacing the implicit universal limit with a typed reliability contract. Keep unknown current distinct from a verified zero-current condition.

Validation should cover pulsed and reversing waveforms, a multi-source tree, a mesh whose current division changes after widening, identical via counts with different access geometry, a narrow pin on a wide route, a heated segment, and a physically continuous line split into arbitrary database edges. Check conservation, units, rounding, and monotonicity: greater required current, temperature, or lifetime must not accidentally relax dimensions under the same model. Compare critical patterns to an independent electrical or field solution, and report both average-density and local-feature margins. After repairs, repeat parasitic and analog checks because reliability improvement can alter speed, coupling, and matching.
