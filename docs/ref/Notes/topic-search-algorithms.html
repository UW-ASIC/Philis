Search algorithms and constraint-aware moves

Compare annealing, constructive search and mathematical methods with explicit guarantees.

Search must protect validity while spending computation on useful alternatives

Analog placement combines discrete choices, continuous dimensions, equality constraints, forbidden geometric regions and expensive electrical evaluations. No single optimizer removes that structure. A useful architecture assigns each kind of decision to a method that can handle it: constructive moves for exact relationships, graph or linear compaction for fixed topology, simulated annealing or enumeration for discrete alternatives, and circuit analysis for deciding whether a geometric improvement matters electrically.

The sources repeatedly separate feasibility from optimization. Lampaert uses constrained groups and performance penalties within annealing; the topological volume develops encodings that avoid spending most of the run rejecting invalid arrangements; Graeb's sizing book derives constrained numerical methods and carefully distinguishes local stationary solutions from global optimization. The engine should preserve these distinctions in its reports. Lampaert, PDF pp. 99–107; Topological Approaches, PDF p. 25; Design Centering, PDF pp. 188–195.

Proposed decision order: first determine whether the candidate is physically and electrically meaningful, then whether required budgets pass, then compare the chosen quality objectives. A cheap proxy may rank candidates for deeper evaluation, but a low proxy value must never erase an open connection, a violated equality or an unsupported model.

Preserve exact constraints with the move itself

Consider a matched pair whose center coordinates satisfy xA + xB = 2a and yA = yB. Moving A independently breaks symmetry; subsequently projecting B can collide with a fixed block. Moving the axis by δ translates both partners by δ and preserves the equality. Changing pair separation by moving A left and B right also preserves it. Swapping both members of two compatible pairs can preserve a complete group. These moves retain useful degrees of freedom without repeatedly leaving the constraint surface.

Group preservation is preferable when a requirement is exact and has a compact parameterization. Projection remains useful for general constraints, but the complete affected component must be rechecked after projection. Sequentially projecting incompatible equalities can cycle; projection against one symmetry axis can invalidate another. Use a simultaneous solve for coupled linear equalities, or explicitly declare which group decomposition is assumed. Fixed devices must participate in that solve rather than being restored afterward without rechecking the equality.

Philis already centralizes detailed-placement trials in Sa::trial (line 140) and exposes project_hard (line 348). This is the correct integration boundary for compound moves and candidate auditing. A trial transaction should include coordinates, orientation, selected geometry variant, pin offsets, shared axes, branch state and every derived cache affected by the mutation. The reason is reproducibility: a rejected reshape must not leave a changed terminal position influencing later scores.

Lexicographic feasibility is clearer than an enormous penalty weight

A finite penalty alone does not establish hard feasibility: improvement in another objective can outweigh a violation unless a sufficient exact-penalty bound has been proved for the modeled problem. Merely increasing an empirical weight supplies no such proof. Even an exact-penalty result about the final optimum does not make every intermediate search state feasible. A lexicographic key first compares hard violation status, then a defined violation magnitude, then required-budget violations, and finally the feasible objective. During an infeasible initialization, this gives a controlled repair policy. Once feasible, accepted moves remain feasible under the checks that define the key.

Philis's detailed placer explicitly compares a gate key containing violated hard batches, hard residual plus encroachment, and budget residual before the Metropolis decision. accept (line 379) therefore expresses stronger intent than a flat weighted sum. However, batch count depends on batching: combining ten requirements into one batch can change a lexicographic count. Use stable semantics for violation counts and ensure aggregate residuals do not hide one worsened mandatory constraint behind another improved one.

Action: distinguish an exploratory infeasibility score from a release predicate. The former may aggregate residuals to find a route back toward legality; the latter checks each mandatory constraint against its own tolerance. This permits useful repair without allowing an aggregate zero or a favorable average to masquerade as complete compliance. Record numerical tolerances in physical units or normalized units with a documented scale.

Some budget tradeoffs are real. Two wire capacitances can share one bandwidth allowance, so requiring both individual caps to decrease may unnecessarily reject a valid move. Other constraints are independent obligations, such as two separate output voltage limits. The constraint system must say which residuals are pooled before the optimizer chooses a gate. See performance budget derivation for the electrical distinction.

What simulated annealing provides, and what it does not

For a fixed scalar energy E and a symmetric proposal distribution, ordinary Metropolis acceptance is one for ΔE ≤ 0 and exp(−ΔE/T) otherwise. Temperature has the same units as E. If the objective changes from nanometer wirelength to a normalized electrical loss, the old temperature schedule is not automatically meaningful. Lampaert discusses estimating initial temperature from typical uphill moves and an intended initial acceptance rate. A useful approximation is T0 = −mean(positive ΔE)/ln(p0); it is a calibration heuristic, not an exact guarantee of the aggregate rate. Lampaert, PDF pp. 117–118.

For deliberately asymmetric proposals, Metropolis–Hastings would include the reverse/forward proposal ratio to target the usual equilibrium distribution. Repair maps can make proposals many-to-one and asymmetric. A practical annealer may still use them as an effective optimization heuristic, but then textbook stationary-distribution or convergence arguments need additional work. Likewise, a finite cooling schedule and finite move budget do not establish global optimality. The asymptotic theorem is not a product guarantee.

Keep the energy definition fixed during an inner annealing epoch. Changing penalties, thermal fields or sensitivity coefficients while comparing a cached previous score with a newly evaluated score means ΔE no longer compares the same function. Philis's Sa::prices field (line 85) explicitly holds prices read-only during the anneal. Extend this discipline to all model revisions: close an epoch, update models, rescore the incumbent and archive, then resume search.

while computation remains:
    freeze a model snapshot and objective scales
    for each trial in this epoch:
        propose an atomic move or coupled branch transition
        generate geometry and preserve exact group relations
        evaluate mandatory constraints and complete gate key
        if gate improves: accept and invalidate affected caches
        if gate ties: apply the documented annealing rule
        otherwise: restore the full prior candidate
    route/extract selected candidates and audit model errors
    refresh models when needed; rescore before the next epoch

Maintain an archive of feasible nondominated candidates as well as the current annealing state. An uphill excursion is useful for exploration but should not destroy the best known deliverable. Compare several seeds using identical time budgets; report distributions of routed electrical outcomes rather than one lucky best placement.

Disconnected legal regions require discrete transitions

Some spacing rules are disjunctions, not lower bounds. In the topological volume's deep-trench example, a pair may share a region within a maximum stretch or be isolated beyond a minimum separation; intermediate gaps are forbidden. If legal gap g satisfies 0 ≤ g ≤ smax or g ≥ d, with d > smax, continuous small steps that never worsen hard legality cannot travel between the two components. No clever temperature rescues a move rejected before the energy test. Topological Approaches, PDF pp. 113–115, p. 136.

Action: propose a branch change together with a legal endpoint geometry. Philis already carries Layout::branch and DtiBand (line 18). Keep branch identity stable across constraint reordering, and verify that the move realizes its declared branch. If one device has left and right sharing stretches, constrain their sum when the physical rule limits total stretch; checking each side independently can permit an impossible total.

A bounded mixed-integer formulation makes the logic explicit. With z ∈ {0,1}, 0 ≤ g ≤ gmax, require g ≥ dz and g ≤ smax + (gmax−smax)z. z = 0 selects sharing; z = 1 selects isolation. The formulation requires gmax ≥ d. Tight bounds improve numerical conditioning. An arbitrarily enormous big-M value can weaken relaxation and hide numerical violations.

Use exact optimization where the model is small enough to justify it

For a fixed ordering and fixed dimensions, minimize width or height subject to linear spacing, symmetry and alignment equations. A feasible LP with an optimality certificate solves that modeled coordinate subproblem. Rectangular area W×H is bilinear, so ordinary linear programming does not directly minimize area unless one dimension is fixed, an alternative linear objective is chosen, or the problem is otherwise reformulated. Enumerating several aspect-ratio bounds is often clearer than pretending a weighted W+H objective is identical to area.

For tiny groups, enumerate orientations, variants or ordering choices and solve the coordinate subproblem for each. Keep the nondominated alternatives. This provides a useful oracle for evaluating a heuristic placer, and can generate reliable templates for recurring motifs. The hierarchical synthesis chapter uses bottom-up variant enumeration and compaction for small clusters, while recognizing that large combinations become expensive. Topological Approaches, PDF pp. 130–147.

Mixed-integer models can select separating directions for rectangles with four binary choices and bounded inequalities. They can also choose contact counts, legal variants and disjunctive branches. Their result is globally optimal only relative to the finite model, domain and solver termination certificate. A time-limited incumbent is a feasible solution with a bound or gap if the solver provides one; an interrupted search with no incumbent does not prove infeasibility.

Continuous performance optimization needs similar discipline. Local linear or quadratic models support useful steps, but a trust region limits how far a step can extrapolate. Evaluate the true model after the step and compare predicted with actual improvement. A good coordinate LP cannot certify a circuit metric computed by an invalid sensitivity model. Design Centering, PDF pp. 174–179.

Experiments that tell whether the search is improving the engine

First use tiny cases with known answers: a two-pair symmetry problem, a three-block packing, and a shared-versus-isolated gap with legal solutions in both components. Enumerate their discrete choices independently. Verify that compound moves can reach both legal branches, that rejected moves restore pin geometry as well as coordinates, and that every recorded incumbent passes direct geometry checks. These tests target the failure mechanisms that matter.

Then compare search variants on identical generated cells, objective scales, PDK rules and evaluation budgets. Report attempted moves, legal proposals, accepted moves, model refreshes, routed completion rate and final electrical margins. A lower placement cost accompanied by more routing failure is a regression. A faster run that silently restricts templates needs its search-domain change reported alongside the speedup.

Finally retain explainable termination states: verified feasible result, feasible result with unresolved electrical model accuracy, budget exhaustion, conflicting constraints, and proven infeasibility of a named subproblem. These states give the next stage enough information to widen the search, relax an explicitly optional preference, or return a justified failure. They prevent a heuristic stopping rule from becoming an unjustified claim of optimality.
