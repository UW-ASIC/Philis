# 2.3 Placement of a Symmetry Group

> **Chapter 2**: Hierarchical Placement with Layout Constraints (Lin & Chang)
> **Part**: Part I: Placement

This section introduces the **Automatically Symmetric-Feasible B\*-tree (ASF-B\*-tree)**, a compact data structure that guarantees valid symmetric placements for symmetry groups in analog layout. The key insight is that by operating only on *representative* modules (one half of each symmetry pair, or one half of each self-symmetric module), the search space is drastically reduced while ensuring that the mirrored placement is always valid.

---

## Key Concepts

### 2.3.1 Automatically Symmetric-Feasible B\*-tree

The ASF-B\*-tree builds on the [[2.2 Review of B-Trees|B\*-tree]] representation (see Section 2.2.3) by introducing the concept of **representatives** to halve the problem size when placing symmetric modules.

#### Representatives

- **Representative of a symmetry pair** $(b_j, b_j')$: The representative $b_j^r$ is defined as $b_j'$ (Definition 2.2). That is, one module from the pair is chosen to represent both.
- **Representative of a self-symmetric module** $b_k^s$: The representative $b_k^r$ is the right half (for vertical symmetry) or top half (for horizontal symmetry) of $b_k^s$ (Definition 2.3). Self-symmetric modules straddle the symmetry axis.
- The number of representatives in a symmetry group equals the number of symmetry pairs plus the number of self-symmetric modules.

#### Representative B\*-tree (Definition 2.4)

A **representative B\*-tree** is a B\*-tree containing only the representative nodes that correspond to representative modules. It encodes only half of the symmetric placement.

#### Mirrored Placement (Definition 2.5)

The **mirrored placement** of the representative modules for a symmetry group $S_i$ places the nonrepresentative modules at the mirrored positions of their representatives with respect to the symmetry axis (axes), using:

$$x = 2\bar{x} - x', \quad y = y' \quad \text{(vertical axis } \bar{x}\text{)}$$

$$x = x', \quad y = 2\bar{y} - y' \quad \text{(horizontal axis } \bar{y}\text{)}$$

where $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$ is the symmetry axis coordinate and $(x', y')$ is the representative's center. For self-symmetric modules, the representative and nonrepresentative halves are **not disjoint** (they share the axis boundary).

#### Symmetric-Feasible Condition (Definition 2.6)

A representative B\*-tree is **symmetric-feasible** if the mirrored placement of the representative modules can be obtained after packing the representative B\*-tree -- i.e., no overlaps occur in the mirrored placement.

#### ASF-B\*-tree (Definition 2.7)

An **ASF-B\*-tree** is a representative B\*-tree that satisfies **Property 2.1** (the boundary constraint). Once packed, coordinates of all symmetric modules are computed via the mirror equations above, and the result is guaranteed to be a valid **symmetry island**.

### Lemmas and Proofs

#### Lemma 2.1: Self-symmetric modules must abut the symmetry axis

The representative of a self-symmetric module must abut the symmetry axis. Since the symmetry axis $\bar{x}$ always passes through the center $(x_s, y_s)$ of a self-symmetric module $b^s$ (i.e., $\bar{x} = x_s$), and the representative $b^r$ is the right half, the center of $b^s$ lies on the left boundary of $b^r$. Therefore $b^r$ must abut the axis.

#### Lemma 2.2: Symmetry pairs not on the axis are always feasible

For a symmetry pair $(b, b')$ not on the symmetry axis, the distance from the axis to each module's center satisfies $|x - \bar{x}| = |\bar{x} - x'| \geq \frac{w}{2}$. Since the modules are on opposite sides of the axis and far enough apart, they never overlap. The symmetric-feasible condition is always satisfied.

#### Property 2.1: Boundary Constraint

- **Vertical symmetry axis**: The representative node of a self-symmetric module must always be on the **rightmost branch** of the representative B\*-tree (because the representative is on the right-half plane and must abut the left boundary of that plane, which is the symmetry axis).
- **Horizontal symmetry axis**: The representative node of a self-symmetric module must always be on the **leftmost branch** of the representative B\*-tree.

### Key Theorems

#### Theorem 2.1: ASF-B\*-tree is symmetric-feasible

An ASF-B\*-tree is symmetric-feasible in a symmetric placement of a symmetry group with respect to either a vertical or a horizontal symmetry axis. This follows directly from Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2, which cover all four kinds of representatives.

#### Theorem 2.2: Packing produces a symmetry island

The packing of an ASF-B\*-tree results in a **symmetry island** of the corresponding symmetry group. After packing the representatives into a connected placement, the symmetry axis is set to the left (or bottom) boundary. The mirrored modules also form a connected placement abutting the axis, so the entire group forms a connected placement where each module abuts at least one other module.

#### Theorem 2.3: Unique correspondence

There exists a **unique correspondence** between a compacted symmetric placement of a symmetry group and its induced ASF-B\*-tree. This follows from the unique correspondence between admissible placements and B\*-trees, combined with the uniqueness of the mirrored placement.

> These theorems are critical because they mean the ASF-B\*-tree avoids searching redundant solution spaces -- every tree corresponds to exactly one valid symmetric placement, and every valid symmetric placement has exactly one tree.

### 2.3.2 ASF-B\*-Tree Packing

The packing algorithm for an ASF-B\*-tree follows the same **preorder tree traversal** procedure as ordinary B\*-tree packing, with an additional mirroring step.

#### Packing Procedure

1. **Traverse the ASF-B\*-tree in preorder** (root, left subtree, right subtree).
2. For each representative node visited, compute its $(x, y)$ coordinate:
   - Left child $n_j$ of $n_i$: $x_j = x_i + w_i$ (placed to the right of parent)
   - Right child $n_k$ of $n_i$: $x_k = x_i$ (placed above parent, same x-coordinate)
   - The y-coordinate is determined by the **contour structure**.
3. Maintain **two doubly-linked lists** for both **horizontal** and **vertical** contour structures during packing.
4. After all representative modules are placed, compute symmetric module coordinates using the mirror equations (2.1) and (2.2) with the symmetry axis coordinates $\bar{x}_i$ and $\bar{y}_i$.

#### Contour Management for Symmetry Islands

After packing representatives, the symmetry island has:
- **One top horizontal contour** and **dual vertical contours** (one on each side of the symmetry axis).

To compute the **bottom horizontal contour** of the symmetry island:
1. Traverse both vertical contours from bottom to top.
2. Identify the **convex points** (points where the contour changes direction outward).
3. Connect the convex points horizontally to form the bottom contour.

This bottom contour is needed when the symmetry island is embedded in the larger [[2.4 The Hierarchical Framework|hierarchical HB\*-tree]] placement.

#### Packing Complexity (Theorem 2.4)

**The packing for an ASF-B\*-tree or an HB\*-tree takes linear time $O(n)$.**

Given a design with $n$ total modules and $m$ symmetry groups:
- Let $\bar{n}$ = number of nonsymmetric modules, $n(S_i)$ = number of modules in symmetry group $S_i$
- Then $n = \bar{n} + \sum_{i=1}^{m} n(S_i)$

The HB\*-tree contains:
- $m$ hierarchy nodes
- $O(\sum_{i=1}^{m} n(S_i))$ contour nodes
- $\bar{n}$ module nodes

**Proof sketch**:
1. Packing the ASF-B\*-tree for $S_i$: $O(n(S_i))$ for representative packing (linear by B\*-tree property) + $O(n(S_i))$ for computing symmetric module coordinates (constant time each) = $O(n(S_i))$.
2. Packing the HB\*-tree: $O(m + \bar{n})$ for non-symmetry nodes, plus $O(n(S_i))$ per symmetry island for bottom contour comparison.
3. Total: $O(m + \sum_{i=1}^{m} n(S_i) + \bar{n}) = O(m + n)$. Since $m \leq n$, the total is $O(n)$.

## Algorithms & Methods

### Algorithm: ASF-B\*-tree Packing

```
Input: ASF-B*-tree T for symmetry group S, symmetry axis coordinates
Output: Coordinates of all modules in S

1. Initialize horizontal and vertical contour lists (doubly-linked lists)
2. Pack T using preorder traversal:
   For each node n_i visited:
     a. If n_i is left child of n_parent: x_i = x_parent + w_parent
     b. If n_i is right child of n_parent: x_i = x_parent
     c. Compute y_i from horizontal contour structure
     d. Update both horizontal and vertical contours
3. For each representative module b_r in T:
     Compute symmetric module coordinates:
       - Vertical axis:   x_sym = 2*x_bar - x_r,  y_sym = y_r
       - Horizontal axis: x_sym = x_r,  y_sym = 2*y_bar - y_r
4. Compute bottom contour of symmetry island:
     a. Traverse dual vertical contours bottom-to-top
     b. Record convex points
     c. Connect convex points horizontally
5. Return module coordinates and island contours
```

**Time complexity**: $O(n(S_i))$ -- linear in the number of modules in the symmetry group.

### Simulated Annealing Perturbation Operations (from Section 2.5)

The ASF-B\*-tree is optimized using simulated annealing with cost function:

$$\Phi(P) = \alpha \cdot A_P + \beta \cdot W_P$$

where $A_P$ is the bounding rectangle area and $W_P$ is the half-perimeter wire length (HPWL).

**ASF-B\*-tree perturbation operations** (must maintain Property 2.1):

| Op | Description | Constraints |
|---|---|---|
| Op1 | Rotate a module | Symmetry pairs: rotate both simultaneously. Self-symmetric: update representative shape. |
| Op2 | Move a node | Symmetry pair rep: move anywhere. Self-symmetric rep: move only along the rightmost/leftmost branch. |
| Op3 | Swap two nodes | Both symmetry pairs: arbitrary swap. If self-symmetric involved: at least one must stay on the boundary branch. |
| Op4 | Change a representative | Swap which module in a pair is the representative. |
| Op5 | Convert a symmetry type | Change between vertical and horizontal symmetry. |

## Connections to Bigger Picture

The ASF-B\*-tree is the foundational data structure that makes the hierarchical placement framework of Chapter 2 possible. It solves the problem of guaranteeing valid symmetric placements without post-hoc checking or constraint solving -- every packing of an ASF-B\*-tree automatically yields a symmetry island. This is critical for analog circuits where matched device pairs (e.g., differential inputs in op-amps) require precise geometric symmetry to minimize offset voltages and improve CMRR. The ASF-B\*-tree feeds into the [[2.4 The Hierarchical Framework|HB\*-tree]] (Section 2.4), where symmetry islands are treated as hierarchy nodes that can be simultaneously placed with nonsymmetric modules. The linear-time packing complexity (Theorem 2.4) is essential for the simulated annealing optimization loop in [[2.5 The Algorithm|Section 2.5]], which evaluates thousands of candidate placements. Compared to constraint-based approaches in other chapters (e.g., [[3. Analog Placement with Topological Symmetry Constraints|Chapter 3]]'s symmetry constraints on sequence pairs), the ASF-B\*-tree approach is constructive rather than declarative -- symmetry is built into the representation itself rather than enforced as an external constraint.
