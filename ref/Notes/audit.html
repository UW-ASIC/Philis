Audit of theory and references

The notes are more accurate and better referenced after this audit. They are not certified “fully accurate” or exhaustive. This review checks the HTML’s analog theory against the supplied PDFs, corrects identified errors and separates source findings from our own derivations and proposed P&R policies.

What was audited

Four reviewers divided the supplied sources and checked the handbook’s technical claims, source locations, model assumptions, numerical examples and diagram meaning. The review covered the 30 teaching chapters and 13 source companions, plus the theory in the constraint catalog and glossary. Pre-existing implementation observations and the engine roadmap’s code claims were excluded; no Philis source-code correctness is certified.

The frozen pre-correction inventory contains 553 in-article PDF-link occurrences. Every occurrence received a disposition. This includes reading maps and navigation, not just theory assertions, and excludes 13 source-header links. Added pinpoint links and the 115 reusable provenance records were checked during correction. Counts below refer to the original inventory so new links cannot inflate the apparent audit coverage.

Auditing a cited assertion is different from reading every line of every book. Books were revisited at the passages supporting the notes; the source companions retain their original reading-depth limits. Broad chapter surveys are qualified or navigation. No source-paper experiments, silicon measurements, foundry qualification or global layout optimality proof were reproduced.

Corrections that matter for implementation

  1. Removed a false source-error claim. The Lampaert penalty equation had been called a printing error. Its rendered Eq. (4.11) is correct; the audit removed that allegation and distinguishes the source formula from alternative penalty policies.
  2. Narrowed incorrect or incomplete references. Examples include the CC review’s MOM structures (Fig. 5, PDF 4), Hastings LOD illustration (Fig. 13.52, PDF 698), substrate buried shields/dual rings, and the actual topological metaverification and route-integration sections.
  3. Corrected classifier interpretation. WAGN’s single-threshold soft classification score is neither a full performance distribution nor evidence of calibrated manufacturing yield. The source definition and training equations now accompany that distinction.
  4. Exposed simplified-model assumptions. Single-branch source degeneration, weighted/ratio moments, RC matrix stamps, charge sharing and toy wire-width optima now identify the derivation and its domain rather than implying the entire result was printed in a cited paper.
  5. Corrected physical qualifications. Dummy validity depends on qualified geometry/bias; current density is cross-sectional; transient tree-current sums include shunt/displacement loads; net terminal energy flow is not always dissipated heat.
  6. Checked apparent source inconsistencies against images and algebra. Hastings Appendix E intermediate signs/prefactors/root, the EM via-temperature multiplier and two centering equations retain explicit qualified corrections. Appendix E also distinguishes total-resistance approximation error from metal-only error. These are apparent internal inconsistencies, not publisher-confirmed errata.
  7. Kept distinct metrics distinct. The DAC paper’s N-dependent settling-frequency model is not the ordinary RC −3 dB pole. Runtime percentages/denominators and reported benchmark tradeoffs are scoped to the actual table or figure.
  8. Added reusable provenance. Each of the 84 proposed constraint families now links to the mechanisms or original policies supporting it. References identify exact source files, sections/equations/figures and assumptions; they do not turn the proposed catalog into a textbook’s own numbered rule system.

Detailed corrections and page reviews: physical-layout textbook · optimization books · physics/reliability sources · matching and performance papers. Independent Appendix E check preserves the boundary-value algebra.

What remains unverified or incomplete

  • Foundational noise citations: no pinpoint Johnson–Nyquist 4kBTR or sampled kT/C passage was found in the supplied PDFs. The glossary flags the former as a background law; the DAC chapter shows the latter’s conditional equipartition derivation. See D07 and D08. Do not attribute these formulas to the DAC/layout papers.
  • Production values and validity: actual process limits, mismatch/correlation coefficients, extraction stacks, device qualification, package/thermal models and reliability rules still come from the target PDK and characterization.
  • Exhaustiveness: specialty RF/high-voltage/JFET/memory topics remain surveys of layout implications. The supplied corpus and this audit do not provide a complete qualified generator for every device or cover every modern process effect.
  • Source authority: book/paper examples are evidence under their assumptions. References cited inside a supplied work are not automatically independently reviewed sources. Apparent errors have not been checked against publisher errata.
  • Implementation and experiments: proposed algorithms, schemas, tests and constraint policies are engineering synthesis. Their implementation correctness and measured benefit have not been established by this notes-only audit.

There are no original citation occurrences left without an audit disposition. That completeness statement concerns the ledger, not an absolute correctness guarantee for the handbook.

How to use the audited notes

Start with the theory records and comment templates. Select the narrow claim matching the implementation, retain its assumptions, and add the design/PDK source for numerical limits. If a record is marked derived or proposed, preserve that wording. Then use the teaching chapter for the causal explanation, tradeoffs and validation cases.

Semantic audit data: summary · original citation ledger · 115 reusable records and 84 mappings. Artifact integrity is a separate check: HTML/link/SVG/example validation. A structurally valid link does not by itself establish that a claim is true.
