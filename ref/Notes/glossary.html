Glossary and units

Use these definitions to keep physical geometry, electrical behavior, optimization claims and verification evidence distinct. The linked chapters contain derivations, source citations, examples and implementation guidance. Terms describe mechanisms; they do not supply process limits.

Units and naming conventions

Carry units and terminal identities through every representation. A geometrically plausible result can be wrong by orders of magnitude after a silent area conversion, multiplicity change or terminal permutation.

QuantityConvention to preserve
Length and area1 μm = 1,000 nm; 1 μm² = 106 nm². Squaring a length conversion is essential. Name the coordinate system and manufacturing grid.
Resistance and conductanceΩ and S = Ω−1. Sheet resistance uses Ω/□; L/W is dimensionless. Contact resistance is not automatically a sheet-resistance term.
Capacitance and chargeF and C. A capacitance matrix entry needs node identities and a sign convention; a positive physical coupling capacitor produces negative off-diagonal nodal entries.
Current and current densityA and A/m², or explicitly stated alternatives. Current per drawn width is a process-specific surrogate rather than cross-sectional current density in A/m².
Temperature and thermal resistanceUse kelvin in physical equations. Temperature differences have the same numerical size in K and °C. K/W requires a stated thermal boundary condition.
Error and noiseState absolute versus fractional error; 1 ppm = 10−6. Distinguish RMS, standard deviation, variance, spectral density and peak excursion.

In this handbook, G, D, S and B name MOS gate, drain, source and body. Array index order is never an electrical definition. State whether W means one finger's width or aggregate width, whether nf counts fingers and whether m replicates a modeled device. Their interpretation is PDK/model-specific.

Intent, optimization and models

Optimality

Best performance under a declared objective, feasible set, scenarios and model. A best observed candidate is not a proof of a global optimum. See optimality.

Pareto dominance

One feasible candidate dominates another when it is no worse in every selected objective and better in at least one. Nondominated candidates expose genuine tradeoffs.

Hard constraint and soft cost

A hard requirement defines acceptability. A soft cost guides preference or search. A penalty's small value does not establish that all hard requirements pass.

Electrical sensitivity

A derivative or measured response of circuit performance to a parameter. It translates geometric or device perturbations into an error budget near a stated operating condition.

Operating scenario

A jointly allowed set of supplies, signals, currents, temperature, sequencing and timing. Independent extrema can create impossible combinations or omit a dangerous permitted transient.

Process design kit (PDK)

The process-specific models, layers, rules, extraction data, cells and associated restrictions used to design for a manufacturing technology. Its exact version matters.

Parameterized cell (PCell)

A generator for a supported device or structure. Legal parameter ranges, recognition, terminal definitions and transform restrictions remain part of its contract.

Qualified primitive

A physical construction supported by applicable characterization and integration restrictions. Being drawable, extractable or nominally simulatable does not alone establish qualification.

Surrogate model

A cheaper approximation used to predict or rank outcomes. Its calibration domain and uncertainty limit optimization authority; final evaluation should use the required evidence.

Bias or operating point

The terminal voltages, currents and device state around which behavior is evaluated. Identical geometry at different bias can have different sensitivity and mismatch.

Geometry and device representation

Drawn and effective dimensions

Drawn dimensions describe mask intent; effective dimensions describe the model's electrically relevant structure after process and bias effects. Avoid double-counting PCell or model corrections.

Active geometry and bounding box

Active geometry contributes to device behavior. A bounding box also includes whitespace, guards and access. Its center or area need not represent electrical contribution.

Topology, connectivity and geometry

Connectivity states what joins what; topology includes branches and shared paths; geometry embeds those paths in layers and coordinates. All influence extracted behavior.

Terminal identity and orientation

Electrical roles must survive rotation, reflection and cell collapse. A geometric mirror can reverse source/drain direction or alter a qualified asymmetric device.

Unitization

Decomposition into physical units with an explicit series/parallel composition and parent-device assignment. Equal unit shapes do not imply equal electrical sensitivity. See layout sizing.

Multiplicity

A model or generator's replication count. Aggregate width is W×nf×m only if those parameters have those meanings; shared fingers can have different parasitics from independent replicas.

Diffusion sharing

Joining compatible device regions to save area or parasitics. It changes contacts, edge context and possible carrier paths; same net names alone do not prove safety.

Well, body and substrate

Physical semiconductor regions with distributed resistance, capacitance and junctions. A schematic body net does not make the entire region an ideal equipotential conductor.

Dummy structure

Material added to control an edge or process environment. Dummies still create fields, capacitance and sometimes devices; their geometry and electrical ties require rules.

Density and fill

Density measures material coverage within defined windows. Fill helps satisfy process requirements but changes parasitics and device environment. Check the final filled geometry.

See fabrication, MOS construction and manufacturability for their physical consequences.

Matching, spatial fields and device environment

Matching

Agreement of specified electrical quantities or their responses, usually differences or ratios. Equal dimensions are one means; bias, temperature and access also matter.

Local random mismatch

Stochastic device-to-device differences remaining under the model's shared conditions. Their variance, spatial correlation and geometry dependence require characterization.

Systematic spatial variation

A position- or environment-dependent field, such as thickness or temperature. Linear gradients are one approximation; nonlinear and discontinuous fields need different treatment.

Common centroid

Equality of normalized first moments for the relevant contributions. It cancels linear-field mismatch under the assumed response model; it does not certify routing or LDE equality.

Electrical weight and moment

A contribution or sensitivity determines how each unit samples a field. The weights depend on circuit composition and whether perturbations are absolute or fractional.

Higher-order moment

A weighted average of coordinate products such as x², xy or y². Matching every relevant monomial cancels the corresponding polynomial-field terms under a linear response.

Covariance

Joint variation between parameters or units. A valid covariance matrix is positive semidefinite; for linearized error e=dᵀδp, its variance is dᵀΣd.

Pelgrom-style area law

A characterized inverse-area variance model, often expressed as σ(ΔVTH)=AVT/√(WL). Pair versus individual-device conventions and validity limits must be explicit.

Layout-dependent effect (LDE)

A device-parameter change caused by surrounding geometry or process context. A zero centroid residual can coexist with substantial LDE mismatch.

Well proximity effect (WPE)

Device changes related to well-implant processing near well edges. Use the PDK's geometry descriptors and model rather than one universal edge distance.

LOD and STI context

Length-of-diffusion and shallow-trench-isolation surroundings can alter stress and transistor behavior. Active-edge distances and dummy arrangements differ from well-edge descriptors.

Thermomechanical stress

Mechanical stress arising from materials, temperature and assembly. Its electrical effect depends on device orientation and process; absolute stress and mismatch-causing stress differences differ.

See gradient matching, random mismatch and thermal placement.

Interconnect, noise and passive behavior

Sheet resistance

Resistance of a square of a uniform conducting film, independent of square size in the ideal model. A strip gives R=R□L/W before access corrections.

Contact, via and access resistance

Resistance through interfaces, vertical connections and current-spreading regions. N cuts need not behave as N equal parallel resistors when access is nonuniform.

Capacitance matrix

A node-resolved relationship between charge and voltage. For linear incremental models, a physical capacitor between nodes adds positive diagonal and negative mutual entries.

Capacitive coupling

Current induced by changing voltage difference across mutual capacitance. Victim voltage depends on impedance, waveform and timing, not only on coupling capacitance.

Charge injection and clock feedthrough

Switch-channel charge redistribution and capacitive transfer of control transitions, respectively. Their effects depend on switching sequence, terminal impedances and the complete capacitance network.

Kelvin and star connections

Kelvin wiring separates force and sense paths at a specified physical boundary. A star controls shared impedance by selecting where branch currents join.

Shield

A deliberately connected conductor that changes field coupling. Its reference impedance, continuity and added loading determine effectiveness; a shield label alone proves nothing.

Thermal noise

Equilibrium fluctuations associated with dissipation. An ideal resistor's classical one-sided open-circuit voltage-noise density is 4kBTR V²/Hz within that model's frequency range. This is a foundational Johnson–Nyquist law; no exact derivation or equation locator was found in the supplied corpus. See the provenance gap and conditional derivation before citing it.

kT/C noise

The voltage variance kBT/C for an ideal capacitor thermalized through resistance. It is not a universal bound for every switched-capacitor topology or correlated sampling scheme.

Quality factor and self-resonance

Q measures reactive storage relative to loss under a stated definition. Parasitic capacitance creates self-resonant behavior; inductance and DC resistance alone are insufficient RF specifications.

See parasitics, signal routing, power/reference paths and RF primitives.

Manufacturing, verification and reliability

DRC

Design-rule checking of declared geometric and derived-layer conditions. Passing means the evaluated deck's checks passed on that geometry, not that circuit performance is guaranteed.

LVS

Layout-versus-schematic comparison of extracted devices and connectivity. Its parameter tolerances, device recognition and treatment of parasitics define the claim it supports.

ERC

Electrical-rule checking, potentially including floating regions, voltage compatibility and prohibited terminal connections. The actual deck and supplied scenarios determine coverage.

PEX

Parasitic extraction of layout-dependent electrical networks or parameters. Accuracy depends on the technology model, geometric interpretation, node mapping and retained frequency/physical effects.

DFM

Design for manufacturability: improving or checking process robustness and yield beyond nominal functional intent. It includes process-specific concerns rather than one generic margin.

Antenna damage

Plasma-induced charging damage to vulnerable dielectrics during fabrication. Checks depend on process-stage connectivity, exposed geometry, oxide family and qualified discharge structures.

Electromigration (EM)

Current-driven atomic transport that can produce voids, extrusions or unacceptable resistance change. Local current, material, temperature, time and boundary conditions govern reliability.

Blech effect

Back-stress can oppose electromigration within a suitably confined diffusion domain. A qualified critical jL condition is not permission to subdivide arbitrary routing segments into “safe” lengths.

IR drop

Voltage differences caused by current through resistance. A branched supply or signal net requires its current distribution; total wire resistance alone is insufficient.

Safe operating area (SOA)

A qualified operating envelope over voltage, current, pulse duration, temperature and relevant device state. Static, switching and reverse-bias conditions can have different limits.

ESD and EOS

Electrostatic discharge is a particular transient stress category; electrical overstress is broader. A qualified ESD rating does not guarantee survival of every sustained fault.

Latchup

Self-sustaining regenerative conduction through parasitic structures, often involving coupled bipolar action. Injection, well/substrate resistance, supply conditions and physical stack affect susceptibility.

Guard ring

A qualified surrounding structure used for contacting, collecting or blocking carriers. Carrier type, junction depth, bias and return impedance determine its actual mechanism.

TDDB

Time-dependent dielectric breakdown under electrical stress. Lifetime evaluation needs the appropriate dielectric, field, area, temperature and stress-history model.

Hot-carrier degradation and bias-temperature instability (BTI)

Distinct stress-induced device-aging mechanisms. Bias, temperature and duty history influence parameter drift; matched initial devices can age differently under unequal conditions.

See verification, EM, protection and substrate isolation.

Circuit metrics and evidence

Figure of merit (FOM)

A selected scalar combination of performance quantities. Normalization, signs and feasibility handling matter; improving FOM can otherwise conceal failure of one specification.

Settling time

Time to enter and remain within a specified error band after a defined event. State loading, stimulus, reference value and whether slew or ringing dominates.

INL

Integral nonlinearity: deviation from a specified ideal or fitted transfer reference, usually in LSB. Endpoint and best-fit conventions can give different values.

DNL

Differential nonlinearity: DAC step size or ADC code-bin width divided by the specified ideal LSB, minus one. Define transitions and endpoint treatment.

CMRR and PSRR

Common-mode and power-supply rejection ratios, with specified input/output referencing and frequency. Device and route imbalance can convert shared disturbances into differential output.

Noise spectral density

Noise power or variance per bandwidth; amplitude density is its square root. State one- versus two-sided convention and integrate with the relevant transfer function.

Offset

The input or output correction needed to reach a specified balance condition. Separate input-referred, output-referred, systematic, random and temperature/aging-dependent components.

Gain, bandwidth and phase margin

Different transfer- and loop-response measures. The selected loop break, operating point and load matter; increased gain or bandwidth alone does not establish stability.

SNDR and SFDR

Signal-to-noise-and-distortion ratio compares signal power to combined unwanted content; spurious-free dynamic range compares it to the largest specified spur. Analysis conventions matter.

Predicted yield

Estimated probability of meeting the complete specification under a declared variation model. Sampling uncertainty and model uncertainty remain separate from measured manufacturing yield.
