Terminal parasitic networks

Model resistance, shunt and mutual capacitance where they affect the circuit.

Parasitic-aware P&R must know which conductor connects which terminals, how resistance is distributed, and where capacitance couples. Length and total capacitance are useful search estimates, but circuit behavior depends on the extracted network and its operating conditions.

What the layout adds to a schematic

A drawn net is not equipotential at all frequencies or currents. Contacts and wires introduce resistance, so different points on the same named net can have different voltages. Each conductor also stores charge relative to surrounding conductors and substrate. Routing can therefore create source degeneration, shift poles and zeros, load references, inject clock edges, or unbalance differential paths.

Derived constraint. Bound the electrical effect on the relevant terminals or performance metrics. A maximum route length is only a surrogate for that constraint under assumed width, layer, topology, and surroundings. Lampaert's performance-driven method relates parasitic changes to circuit specifications; the wire-sizing paper demonstrates that width changes can alter performance even at a fixed placement. Resistance: material, shape, contacts, and topology

Uniform rectangular wire approximation:
Rwire = R□ · L/W = ρL/(tW).
Series path: Rpath = Σ Rwire + Σ Rvia + Σ Rcontact.

R□ is sheet resistance, L and W must use the same length unit, ρ is resistivity, and t is effective conductive thickness. The formula assumes a reasonably uniform strip and material model. §2.5.3.2, Eq. (2.33), sheet-resistance law, PDF p. 51 / printed p. 38 Width-dependent resistivity, liners, corners, access spreading, contact crowding, and temperature can invalidate a constant-R□ approximation. Use characterized values when the PDK supplies them.

A series path sum is valid for a single path. In a branched or meshed net, solve the conductance network: current can split, and resistance between two terminals depends on all available paths and boundary conditions. “Total resistance on the net” is not generally the resistance relevant to a differential source path, a supply sink, or a DAC charging branch.

Constraints and enforcement. Preserve segment endpoints and layer/type; characterize contacts and vias separately; constrain terminal voltage drop or effective impedance where possible. Use layer-aware path estimates during routing, followed by network extraction. When a via array is added, include access resistance and unequal current sharing rather than assuming N perfectly equal parallel resistors. See EM for why the same topology also matters to reliability.

Capacitance is a relation between conductors

Distinguish intended device capacitance from interconnect parasitics. Ground/substrate capacitance, same-layer lateral coupling, cross-layer overlap/fringe coupling, and capacitor terminal parasitics have different circuit meanings. A parallel run's coupling depends on edge gap, overlap length, layer stack, dielectric geometry, and shields, not just whether the two net names differ.

The following matrix stamp is our circuit-law derivation: a two-terminal linear capacitor obeys qᵢ=c(vᵢ−vⱼ), qⱼ=−qᵢ, and terminal current is the time derivative of charge. For a positive physical capacitor c between nodes i and j, the nodal capacitance matrix receives +c on Cᵢᵢ and Cⱼⱼ, and −c on Cᵢⱼ and Cⱼᵢ. The off-diagonal matrix entry is therefore negative even though the physical coupling capacitor is positive. Store the convention explicitly; mixing these conventions can invert noise predictions or double-count coupling.

Linear interconnect network:
Gv(t) + C dv(t)/dt = i(t).
Frequency domain: (G + jωC)v = i.
These equations follow by summing the resistor and capacitor branch currents with Kirchhoff’s current law. They describe a linear RC network with defined reference/boundary nodes; ideal voltage sources or inductors require additional variables or boundary treatment. The circuit devices and their operating-point models connect to these terminals.

Reason to keep terminal identities. Top-to-substrate capacitance in a DAC can reduce gain, while bottom-plate loading mainly burdens a reference/switch under the paper's ideal static assumptions. Merging both into one total-C penalty loses that distinction. Parasitic classes and their roles, PDF pp. 2–4

Worked example: two widths, two different bottlenecks

Take an illustrative 100 µm route with R□=0.1 Ω/□. At W=0.5 µm, R=20 Ω; at W=1 µm, R=10 Ω. Suppose the corresponding shunt capacitances are 10 and 16 fF from an assumed extraction table. These are teaching numbers, not a PDK recommendation.

If this route is a source lead for a device with gₘ=5 mS, our single-branch derivation i=gₘ(vin−iRₛ) gives gₘ,eff≈gₘ/(1+gₘRₛ), neglecting body effect/output conductance and equating source-resistor current with the modeled branch current. It changes from about 4.55 mS to 4.76 mS as resistance halves. This suggests widening could help that branch, subject to the rest of the circuit.

In a different circuit position, if the added capacitance loads a 100 kΩ node, the corresponding simple pole from this capacitance alone changes from about 159 MHz to 99.5 MHz. Widening now worsens that pole. These are separate illustrative models, not two independent predictions that can simply be added in a real OTA. Their purpose is to show why the constraint must identify the electrical path.

P&R implication. Classify resistance-sensitive, capacitance-sensitive, and coupled RC-sensitive paths using simulation or a validated model. Optimize width and topology jointly with their actual effects. The wire-sizing paper's real circuit examples show the same competing tendency. Table 7, PDF p. 19

Build the extracted object that the constraints need

  1. Establish connectivity and device terminals first. An RC network extracted from mislabeled or shorted geometry cannot validate the intended circuit.
  2. Split conductors at contacts, junctions, branches, and changes of width/layer. This preserves where voltage drops and branch currents occur.
  3. Extract resistance and coupling using the appropriate process model. Include internal macro wiring and pin access; external route-only extraction misses hidden bottlenecks.
  4. Attach parasitics to terminal-resolved nodes. Keep matched sides and capacitor plates distinct, even if they eventually connect to one schematic net.
  5. Reduce the network only with a known error bound or validation. A reduction appropriate for DC IR drop may not preserve frequency-dependent coupling or settling.
  6. Simulate with the original devices and the extracted network. Re-evaluate bias when voltage drops or LDEs change it; do not freeze a schematic operating point beyond its justified range.

Why reduction needs care. Eliminating internal nodes in a resistor network can preserve terminal DC behavior. Eliminating an RC network is frequency dependent; independently collapsing all R and C into a total often changes its poles and transfer functions. Validate the reduced model over the frequency and time domain that matters to the specification.

A fast model should report uncertainty or its applicability domain. When a change crosses a layer boundary, adds a shield, or changes route topology, reevaluate rather than extrapolating an old length-to-C conversion. Calibrate model error against extracted results and use that error to choose meaningful reserves.

Electrical route matching as a multiport constraint

For a differential structure, relevant comparisons may include source resistance, drain load, gate connection resistance, coupling to common aggressors, and frequency-dependent input/output impedance. Equal total length and equal sets of layers are necessary only for some chosen construction methods; they are not a sufficient electrical matching condition.

A useful intermediate signature includes per-layer lengths and widths, via types/counts, branch topology, corresponding terminal R, and selected C matrix entries. The final condition should be derived from the allowed differential error: |Rpos−Rneg|≤ΔRmax, |Cpos,k−Cneg,k|≤ΔCmax,k, or directly a maximum simulated offset/CMRR/settling imbalance. Derive the limits from circuit sensitivity rather than assigning one percentage to every path.

Tradeoff. Adding length to the faster or lower-C side can improve one balance metric while worsening both paths' speed and noise. Prefer a more balanced topology, layer allocation, or access structure when it achieves matching with less common degradation.

Current Philis models and the next constraints

routing lowers a class-derived capacitance allowance to a drawn-length cap using one wire capacitance-per-length value. That is more specific than the fallback in ParasiticBudget::extract; document the actual active path. Extend it with layer, width, via, and topology information, then retain the original electrical allowance for extracted validation.

CouplingBudget usefully sums coupling across multiple aggressors, but its estimate is same-layer and uses one stack-wide coefficient. Cross-layer coupling, fringing, shielding, and excitation are outside that formula. Differential compares total length and a deduplicated layer set; it does not perform RC matching.

harvest currently assigns total extracted capacitance to the final cost. Preserve that diagnostic, but add terminal-resolved metrics and circuit performance. A larger total-C candidate may be better if it substantially reduces a critical resistance or balances a sensitive path.

Validate the model before strengthening the constraint

Use simple structures with independently calculable behavior: one rectangle, series widths, a T branch, a via stack, a via array, overlapping layers, adjacent parallel wires, a grounded shield, and a floating conductor. Check unit conversions, resistance monotonicity, reciprocity/passivity where applicable, connectivity, and geometric invariance under translation.

Then compare representative generated layouts against an independent extractor using the same conductor/device interpretation. The existing PEX cross-check script provides a starting point; agreement in total C alone is not enough if the new constraints depend on individual terminal couplings. Record per-path errors and the circuit-metric error they cause. Increase optimization authority only after the model predicts the distinctions the engine will rely on.
