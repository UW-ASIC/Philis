Circuit intent and physical identity

Carry circuit roles, device units, terminal labels and authorized transformations through P&R.

An analog layout engine must preserve electrical meaning while transforming a netlist into physical objects. Before deciding where devices go, determine what each terminal does, what must match, which devices may share material, and what assumptions justify the constraints.

Recognize a circuit role, then explain its consequence

A differential pair is valuable to recognize because differential behavior depends on balanced devices and paths. A current mirror is valuable to recognize because current ratios depend on device parameters, bias, and connection resistance. A series stack suggests proximity and an ordered connection path, but it does not automatically imply that its devices should occupy a common centroid. A clock net suggests switching activity and aggressor potential, but only the waveform and victim transfer path determine the resulting noise.

Technique. Use terminal-labeled connectivity and device types to identify candidate motifs. Validate geometry, bias domains, body connections, and the surrounding circuit before turning those motifs into physical instructions. Hierarchical recognition reduces ambiguity by using both local patterns and the parent function. See the constraint-system chapter and the topological survey for structural constraints and their transformations.

The diagram deliberately separates a recognized motif from a layout action. The same motif can justify multiple constraints, and some of those constraints may be alternatives. Keep the derivation instead of storing an unexplained boolean “matched.”

Keep three identities: electrical device, unit, and macro

An electrical transistor may be implemented as many fingers, a resistor as many series segments, and a capacitor as many parallel units. Several electrical devices can then be combined in one physical macro. The identities are related, but they are not interchangeable.

IdentityInformation it must retainFailure if discarded
Schematic deviceDevice model, parameters, terminal names/nets, ratio and circuit role.A physically valid array may realize the wrong W/L, multiplicity, series/parallel connection, or bias domain.
Physical unitParent device, active footprint, local coordinates, orientation, neighbors, unit terminals and internal route.Collapsing to a macro center can erase real gradient, thermal, or routing mismatch.
Macro candidateExternal pins, obstacles, legal orientations, variants, certified internal invariants, extraction model.The global placer may choose an orientation or route access that invalidates the macro's assumptions.

Reason for this representation. A global coordinate transformation should move all of a macro's units while preserving their relative geometry. A change of internal pattern, however, changes the matching certificate and route graph. The engine needs to know which kind of transformation happened.

Derive units from circuit quantities

Suppose a current mirror requires 1:4 nominal strength using identical gate length and unit width. Implementing it with one versus four equal units can preserve the intended geometrical ratio, but the bias and connection resistance still need evaluation. If the source regions carry different nets, sharing diffusion is electrically illegal even if the DRC checker sees a perfectly legal polygon. If the transistor model is asymmetric, reversing source and drain may change behavior.

For a resistor ladder, a 1:4 ratio built from identical series segments has a different electrical composition from parallel resistor segments. For capacitors, parallel units add capacitance; series units add reciprocally and change internal node behavior. Unit counts are therefore a typed electrical construction, not a generic multiplier.

Action. Give each unitization a declared composition law, terminal map, exact integer ratio when applicable, and a method to reconstruct the schematic equivalent. Test. Flatten the generated macro and compare it to the intended device relation, including dimensions and intermediate nets. Do this before using matching or area estimates to select a variant.

Specify what kind of symmetry is intended

Distinguish geometric mirror symmetry, common-centroid moment equality, orientation balance, matched route impedance, and equal environment. They often support each other, but none implies all the others. A rectangular mirror of a transistor may swap source/drain orientation. A CC ABBA array can have unequal diffusion-edge distances. Two mirrored routes can see different neighboring aggressors.

Technique. State symmetry constraints at the level of their purpose: which objects correspond, what transformation maps them, which terminals must map, and which exceptions are permitted. A symmetry group should be local to its relevant circuit structure; forcing an unrelated entire chip onto one axis can create long or crossed routes without an electrical benefit.

The CC review's small-device examples demonstrate why a recognized matched pair should not automatically force a single pattern. Generate candidate constructions, then evaluate their LDE, parasitic, and variation effects. §V, PDF pp. 6–7

A constraint needs a reason and provenance

A useful derived constraint answers seven questions: what requirement it protects; what physical mechanism links the two; which objects and terminals it applies to; what process or simulation evidence sets its limit; how it is enforced; when it is inapplicable; and how it is validated. Without those answers, adding more constraints can make search less successful while giving no better circuit.

Worked example. “Keep net X under 10 fF” is incomplete. A justified version might say: in the specified closed-loop OTA testbench, a local perturbation around the present extracted circuit gives a settling-time sensitivity of a particular value; the remaining settling margin allocates an estimated capacitance allowance to X; the allowance applies only in that model's trust region and must be refreshed when the route topology changes. The actual numerical value must come from the design, not this example.

Reason. This records the connection between a proxy and a circuit requirement, permits conflict resolution, and prevents a stale derived limit from surviving a topology or bias change. Performance budgets develops the mathematics.

Handle recognition uncertainty and explicit intent

Structural recognition can misclassify a device pair, and net names can be misleading. A signal named “vdd_sense” may be a sensitive measurement rather than a power trunk. A shared source is evidence for a differential pair, but the surrounding switching phases can matter. Treat inferred role, explicitly declared role, and measured behavior as distinct evidence.

Procedure. Preserve user overrides; record each motif match and its confidence/reason; provide an explanation for every emitted rule; and reject contradictory hard instructions with a localized conflict report. Do not silently drop one of two constraints because it was encountered later. Where intent remains uncertain, use soft guidance or multiple candidates until electrical validation resolves the choice.

Philis already has recognition overrides in AnnotationConfig. The improvement is to expose and propagate why a classification or constraint was produced, so later validation can refer back to it.

Current Philis behavior to preserve and extend

annotate builds recognized blocks, recovers primitive children, derives net classes, and assembles placement, routing, and cell constraints. placement distinguishes paired primitives from stacks, and puts matching/centroid guidance in the cost arm. enumerate filters merged candidates with shared-pad or gate-strap shorts. These are useful existing foundations.

Preserve the original device→unit→macro mapping through retargeting. Add structured reasons when a merged pattern is rejected and a device is emitted separately. Recompute or invalidate certificates when variants, dimensions, orientations, or internal connections change. Otherwise a constraint can remain syntactically present while no longer describing the geometry being optimized.

Validation cases that teach the contract

  1. Permute internal terminal storage while preserving explicit terminal labels and connectivity: role recognition and antenna attribution should remain correct or reject the unsupported representation explicitly. Do not permute positional SPICE terminals and assume the circuit is unchanged.
  2. Make a same-size transistor pair with different body domains: the generator must not merge wells or taps illegally.
  3. Give two fingers different source nets: diffusion sharing must be rejected even if the resulting polygon would pass spacing rules.
  4. Collapse an ABBA group into a macro, rotate it, and verify its internal moments and terminal map still evaluate correctly.
  5. Use a ratioed resistor/capacitor example with series and parallel alternatives: only the electrically correct composition may satisfy the reference netlist.
  6. Provide an explicit do-not-identify override: no downstream stage should resurrect the forbidden inferred grouping.

These cases are discriminating because they target information loss at stage boundaries. They do not merely test whether a known pattern name produces a known rectangle.
