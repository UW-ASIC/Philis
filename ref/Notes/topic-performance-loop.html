The post-layout performance feedback loop

Extract, simulate, update budgets and preserve feasible tradeoffs with reproducible evidence.

A constraint-driven engine becomes performance-driven when every proxy can be traced to a circuit requirement, and promising layouts are judged using the realized circuit. The feedback loop should explain which physical mechanism caused a failed specification and what kind of change can improve it.

Make the circuit specification executable

A schematic alone does not define the layout objective. Define the operating supplies, bias conditions, loads, modes, process/temperature range, testbenches and measurement definitions. “Bandwidth” can mean unity-gain frequency, a closed-loop −3 dB bandwidth, or a settling requirement. “Offset” can mean nominal systematic offset, a mismatch standard deviation or a tail quantile. The optimizer must know which quantity and which scenario are constrained.

For each upper-bounded metric fi, store a residual ri(L,s) = [fi(L,s)−ui]/qi, with a positive scale qi tied to the allocation or tolerance. For lower bounds reverse the sign. The deterministic constraint is maxs∈S ri ≤ 0 over required scenarios S. A statistical specification adds a probability requirement over characterized random variables; a few deterministic corners are not a yield estimate. See robust design.

A nominally attractive result with failed phase margin is infeasible if phase margin is a requirement. Keep feasibility separate from objective weighting. Once all required constraints pass, rank the remaining area, power, performance and runtime tradeoffs according to the declared design policy. This is the bridge between optimality and implementation. Performance-driven motivation, PDF pp. 1–3 Layout objective, PDF p. 20

A low-fidelity rejection must itself be justified. For a requirement f ≤ U, a proven upper bound at or below U certifies a pass, while a proven lower bound above U certifies a failure. An upper bound above U alone does not prove failure; an uncertain heuristic should prioritize evaluation, not silently prove infeasibility. Store model domain and uncertainty with each result. If two candidates are close relative to model error, promote both instead of assigning false precision to their ordering.

Translate performance margin into useful physical budgets

Near a characterized point p₀, approximate a metric as f(p₀+Δp) ≈ f(p₀)+JΔp, where p includes selected parasitic R/C, device parameters or local effects. Estimate derivatives analytically or with controlled perturbation of the extracted/testbench model. Keep the signs: added source resistance may reduce a transconductance-related metric, whereas added compensation capacitance can improve one stability measure while hurting speed.

Consider an illustrative requirement allowing at most 10 mV of extra error. Two layout quantities x and y contribute approximately 2x+y mV in the valid local domain. The shared constraint is 2x+y ≤ 10. Independent caps x≤2 and y≤4 only use 8 mV of that allocation at their corner, and reject x=3,y=2 even though its predicted error is also 8 mV. Conversely, assigning each variable the entire 10 mV budget admits combinations exceeding the total. This is why shared performance constraints are better than arbitrary independent limits when reliable sensitivities exist.

The example is a derivation of budget coupling, not a calibrated Philis circuit. Performance budgets develops the full method from Lampaert. Use a trust region around the characterized operating point, then re-extract and re-simulate after meaningful geometry changes. Near a mode transition, saturation boundary, oscillation onset or disconnected route, a linear model may be invalid. Objective and sensitivity-derived bounds, PDF pp. 34–42

Make feedback actionable: report “output-node capacitance consumed 80% of the modeled bandwidth margin” with the model's scope, not just “cost rose.” Local sensitivity is still an attribution estimate. Validate an intervention by changing that feature and observing the full circuit; interactions may reverse a predicted benefit.

Choose search variables that can reach the needed improvement

A net-weight optimizer searches the layouts produced by its P&R generator. It cannot discover a missing capacitor-array topology or a device orientation that the generator excludes. A width optimizer cannot repair a floorplan that leaves no legal route corridor. Therefore expose several levels of decisions: macro pattern and aspect ratio, relative topology, allowed orientation, route topology/layer/width, and, only if circuit-authorized, sizing.

Use a staged or hierarchical search because the Cartesian product is large. Retain several placement alternatives through routing; route-aware evaluation may reverse a placement-only ranking. Within a promising topology, optimize discrete widths or layer choices; if electrical limits remain unreachable, reopen placement or an authorized sizing level. Distinguish “no feasible candidate found within this search” from “the specified constraints are mathematically inconsistent.” Optimization and prediction approaches, PDF pp. 2–3 Chapter 3, §§3.4–3.5, hierarchical alternatives, PDF pp. 137–147 Chapter 4, §4.5.4, placement/global-route integration, PDF pp. 191–193

Every candidate needs a reproducible record: schematic and elaboration hashes, unit/macro choices, coordinates and transforms, route graph and geometry, rule/model versions, seed, testbench, simulation status and metric vector. This record lets the engine revisit a candidate after budgets or models change without confusing stale predictions with fresh measurements.

Preserve feasible alternatives instead of hiding them in one scalar

Candidate A dominates B for minimization objectives if A is no worse in every required objective and strictly better in at least one. Apply that comparison among candidates passing the same required constraints and evaluated under compatible assumptions. A failed high-speed layout does not dominate a feasible slower one. Uncertain or missing metrics should prevent a confident dominance claim.

Retain a bounded nondominated archive over the relevant metrics. Choose the final layout by an explicit policy, such as minimum area subject to bandwidth, noise, offset, reliability and yield margins. A weighted score is useful when weights have a design meaning, but it may miss alternatives on a nonconvex frontier and can conceal units or feasibility if used carelessly. The observed archive is not a proof of the global frontier. Multiobjective discussion, PDF pp. 4–5

Report measured margin to each required limit, not only a figure of merit. For example, a gain×bandwidth/power objective can rise while phase margin or output swing becomes unacceptable. A normalized objective with an undocumented denominator can also make results incomparable across different circuits. Use circuit-specific objectives and compare methods under identical definitions.

Use learned models as accountable evaluators

The wire-sizing manuscript demonstrates one useful pattern: learn a graph-based classifier at a chosen performance threshold, cheaply propose width assignments, then route/extract/simulate finalists. It retains connectivity, device and routing features because those affect behavior. The manuscript denotes its soft classification output P(FOM<T) for a user-specified threshold. A single-threshold score supplies neither a full cumulative distribution nor a calibrated manufacturing-failure probability. It also differs from FOM regression. Preserve these distinctions in the evaluator interface. §4.2, graph features, PDF p. 8 §4.3–4.5, Eqs. (17)–(19), classification output/training, PDF pp. 10–11

A surrogate needs a domain declaration: PDK, circuit topology/family, sizes, legal route widths/layers, bias modes and training distribution. Measure error separately for each required metric, especially false-feasible predictions near a limit. Held-out random variants of one circuit establish less than held-out topologies or processes. Estimate uncertainty and fall back to simulation when the candidate leaves the validated domain.

Begin with simple sensitivity or regression baselines. Record the cost of obtaining labels as well as training and inference. Cached evaluations and active selection of informative candidates can matter more than a larger model. In the supplied survey the runtime chart and prose have inconsistent percentages; the defensible lesson is to measure Philis's own end-to-end cost, not import the reported fractions. Data and transfer costs, PDF pp. 3–5

An optimizer can exploit model errors, particularly when it makes many queries or proposes shapes unlike the training data. Validate the selected candidates with the actual extractor/testbench, and periodically evaluate candidates the model dislikes to detect blind spots. Keep final verification data separate from iterative fitting when estimating honest generalization.

Build on the checks Philis already performs

The current library flow already runs drawn-geometry DRC/ERC/LVS/PEX scoring within its epoch loop, then selects through lex_key. This is valuable feedback; the missing extension is a complete circuit-performance result vector and its required scenarios. The current final scalar uses signoff's extracted total capacitance. Total capacitance cannot distinguish two equal-C layouts whose capacitance lies on different electrical nodes.

Preserve the existing early checks while adding a performance-evaluation boundary after usable extraction. Do not call a failed or unsupported extraction a zero-parasitic result. Cache only when geometry, PDK/model, extraction options and testbench identities match. Add result states for failure, unknown applicability, insufficient coverage and successful evaluation. Missing operating-point current also must not certify EM or thermal feasibility.

A practical first milestone is an OTA with fixed schematic and qualified testbenches. Compare simple generated alternatives using gain, UGF, phase margin, input noise, offset, power and area under stated conditions. Then introduce one new search dimension at a time and explain its causal result. The handbook's roadmap orders infrastructure ahead of more elaborate search because optimization only helps when its evaluator answers the right question.

State what completion and optimality mean

A run can stop on a compute budget, lack of improvement, exhaustion of a finite candidate set or a proof-bound criterion. Record which occurred. “Best verified candidate after 200 evaluations” is an honest result. “Optimal layout” requires an explicit feasible set, objective and proof or stated approximation meaning. For practical analog P&R, the immediate target is a reproducibly feasible, high-quality layout with explainable margins and evidence that the search improved it.

Acceptance experiments should include an equal-total-C but different-node-loading pair; a candidate that improves bandwidth while failing phase margin; a layout that defeats a local sensitivity model; a stale-cache identity change; and a surrogate that is uncertain outside its circuit family. These discriminate a true performance loop from a renamed geometric score.
