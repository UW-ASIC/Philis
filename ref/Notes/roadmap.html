Handbook reference

An implementation roadmap for Philis

The highest-value changes are the ones that make constraint meaning and evaluation trustworthy, then use that evidence to improve placement and routing. This roadmap maps the supplied literature to the current Philis code; it proposes implementation work and does not claim those extensions are already present.

Code-review scope and the existing foundation

The reviewed workspace is based on commit dad330c42f129cb0bca51427d308745f2d168324, with the user's existing uncommitted changes included in the inspection. The handbook did not modify engine code. Paths and symbol names below describe this snapshot; later changes may supersede the observations. The full research inventory is in source coverage.

Philis already separates hard constraints, budget residuals and cost; recognizes analog structures; creates legal cell variants and some merged matched macros; checks route and placement properties; and evaluates drawn-geometry verification during its epoch loop. The runtime assigns supply/ground net classes to the detailed router. These are existing capabilities to extend, not missing features inferred from older documentation.

The source consensus suggests three improvements in sequence: preserve the electrical meaning of each physical object and rule; make model coverage and final-circuit evaluation explicit; then expand the search space and optimize using those trustworthy results. The sequence follows dependencies, not a claim that any one topic is universally more important for every circuit.

Second: preserve internal units and characterize matching models

CentroidGroup::centroid weights cell centers by bounding-box area, while retargeting maps devices into merged cells. This is a geometric proxy unless the generator guarantees that outline area represents the intended electrical weights and preserves all internal-unit positions. Device matching needs active-unit moments and orientation/environment descriptors, including when several schematic members share one macro.

Proposed interface. A generated macro should expose each physical unit's schematic owner, electrical weight, active polygon or moments, terminal mapping, legal orientation, process environment and route-access contract. Rotate/reflect those descriptors together with geometry. Keep original device and bit identities through merging. The first acceptance test changes only the halo or aspect of an enclosing box: electrical moments should stay fixed if internal active geometry stays fixed.

MatchingPair uses a fixed gradient-to-area-coefficient ratio. The supplied Pelgrom and Drennan sources do not establish a universal process-independent constant for that ratio or a universal 1/√area law over all geometry and bias domains. Add PDK/model-qualified coefficients and their domains, then propagate variation through the actual matched electrical quantity. Use spatial covariance when available and expose uncertain coefficients as assumptions. See random mismatch and gradient matching.

Current annotation registers matching, proximity and centroid terms as costs, while selected symmetry and isolation constraints have stronger roles. Do not describe every existing geometric preference as a hard certificate. Allow circuit-specific required offset/ratio constraints to motivate justified geometric budgets, while preserving alternatives when a costly centroid pattern is unnecessary or electrically worse.

Build family-specific environment descriptors after the unit mapping is sound: MOS LOD/well/poly/dummy environment; resistor contacts, bends and thermoelectric symmetry; capacitor perimeter/plate environment; bipolar emitter/base/collector geometry and temperature. The device chapters explain why a single “matched device” abstraction needs qualified family details.

Third: make parasitic evaluation terminal-aware and close the circuit loop

Verification currently uses total extracted capacitance as its cost, and lex_key uses it in epoch selection after violation tiers. Preserve this useful extraction path but add an electrical result vector under declared testbenches. Two layouts with equal total C can have very different pole locations, offset, settling or noise when the capacitance is on different nodes.

The active parasitic budget construction converts a class/load-derived capacitance allowance into a length bound using per-length wire capacitance. That is already more informative than an arbitrary fixed length. Improve it by retaining the stack/layer/width and terminal-network assumptions, then update local performance sensitivities after meaningful layout changes. Shared circuit margins should constrain combinations of parasitics, rather than independently giving every net the entire allowance.

A first integration target is one fixed-schematic OTA with qualified operating conditions, loads and gain/UGF/phase-margin/noise/offset/power measurements. Archive several feasible candidates and their margins. Cache by geometry, extraction settings, model and testbench identity. Promote uncertain candidates to higher-fidelity evaluation; do not let a cheap estimate certify what it cannot observe. See the performance loop.

Acceptance. A pair of equal-total-C layouts with different node loading should be distinguished electrically. A layout with improved UGF but failed required phase margin should be rejected. Changes to a load or model must invalidate cached results. Only after these work should a surrogate or Bayesian optimizer be judged by how cheaply it finds better verified candidates.

Do not optimize these dimensions independently when they share resources. A shield consumes a track and adds load; wider power wires change nearby coupling; more dispersed matched units create internal route demand. Each mutation should invalidate the affected geometry and performance checks, then return a clear reason for acceptance or rejection.

Fifth: connect current, power, isolation and reliability models

The library already populates supply_nets from annotated supply/ground classes. The reviewed runtime did not show an assignment of per-net operating currents into the detailed router's net_current_ua vector. The detailed router contains a fixed EM_UA_PER_UM rule-of-thumb constant. A meaningful EM contract needs layer/process/temperature/lifetime-qualified capacity and currents for branches, vias and bottlenecks, not only a supply class or net-wide width preference.

Use the appropriate DC/average, RMS and peak information under the PDK rule's semantics. Propagate current through actual route branches and verify current crowding and unequal via sharing where relevant. Iterate electrical heating with temperature-sensitive capacity when required. Keep Blech or other stress-based exemptions conditional on their physical applicability. The EM chapter and power chapter derive the reasons.

The thermal estimate uses superposed half-space point sources with an effective near-field radius and fixed silicon conductivity. It is useful as a placement estimate but lacks detailed stack/package boundaries and transient heat flow. Calibrate its domain; attach confidence; promote sensitive candidates to a qualified model. Preserve the distinction between power from a real operating-point analysis and a fallback/probe assumption. Validate differential temperature at matched devices, not just distance from a hot box.

Substrate isolation similarly needs injection/collection contacts, substrate layers and electrical return paths. Guard geometry with a weak or shared noisy return can provide less isolation than its shape suggests. Add a contact-network/transfer model at a useful fidelity and distinguish latch-up, substrate-noise and ESD mechanisms. The same ring shape does not automatically solve all three.

Sixth: specialized generators and manufacturing closure

A dedicated capacitor-array generator should preserve bit/unit identities, odd-count centroid residuals, separate plate graphs and bridge semantics, then enumerate spiral/chessboard/block alternatives. This is a new capability alongside the current merged plate/comb primitive, not a relabeling of it. Its acceptance criteria are connectivity, code INL/DNL, worst-bit settling and qualified manufacturing rules. See DAC arrays.

Device sizing or refolding should become a search dimension only with an explicit equivalence contract and synchronized reference netlist. The MOS generator intentionally preserves schematic finger information and restricts patterns that would short terminals. Expand its choices through verified generators, not arbitrary permutations. New RF, high-voltage, bipolar or specialty structures similarly need qualified device models, extraction and recognition before optimization can claim their behavior.

Close the manufacturing loop with voltage-dependent spacing, density/fill, antenna stage checks, redundant contacts, qualified dummy/tie rules, package/ESD interfaces and final geometry verification as applicable to the target process. Re-extract after parasitic-affecting repairs. Track exceptions as explicit approved design choices, not silently relaxed penalties. Foundry-specific numbers remain required inputs; the textbooks explain mechanisms and techniques but do not replace a PDK.

Milestones that demonstrate better layouts

  1. Trustworthy certificates: terminal identities, model availability and checker coverage are correct and visible.
  2. Trustworthy matching: emitted unit geometry and characterized statistics predict the intended ratio/offset behavior within stated limits.
  3. Trustworthy electrical selection: candidate ranking respects required post-layout circuit specifications, with reproducible testbenches.
  4. Broader feasible search: route-aware placement, selective widths and specialized generators improve measured margins or tradeoffs at a reported compute cost.
  5. Robust closure: required variation, mission and manufacturing checks apply to the final emitted geometry, with remaining uncertainty named.

Use the experiments in validation to demonstrate these milestones. The intended outcome is an engine that can explain why a constraint exists, which physical quantity it controls, where its model applies, and what evidence makes the selected layout acceptable.
