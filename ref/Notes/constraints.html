The constraint catalog

This proposed catalog contains 84 distinct requirement families organized by physical mechanism. It is a contract and navigation index for extending Philis, not a claim that every rule is implemented, universally applicable, or sufficient for signoff. Each instantiated rule needs process-specific values, circuit context and evidence.

Every rule’s theory mapping identifies the exact supporting mechanisms and which parts are original handbook policy. Reference IDs are source records, not claims that the books define these 84 rule IDs.

How to read and instantiate the catalog

L denotes mandatory physical/process legality once the applicable PDK rule is selected. E denotes a required electrical, reliability or statistical acceptance condition in the design contract. C denotes an exact construction or representation invariant chosen to implement intent. B denotes a derived budget or screening proxy whose assumptions must remain valid. P denotes a preference among acceptable alternatives. A row may contain several roles; for example, required offset is E, a derived centroid tolerance is B, and an explicitly selected exact common-centroid pattern is C. Assigning a search tier does not change the underlying physical meaning.

Every instance should carry a stable ID, targets and scope, units, scenarios, numerical bounds/tolerances, parent requirement, source and model revision, derivation, and current evidence. Outcomes are pass, fail, unknown, or not applicable. Missing current, temperature, extraction or process data normally makes the dependent check unknown. Not applicable requires a justified scope condition; it cannot mean “the tool does not implement this.”

Predicates below are templates, not numerical design rules. Symbols such as limit, tolerance, legal set and supported domain must be instantiated from the design contract or PDK. Evaluate coupled requirements together when they share a performance allowance. Preserve the parent requirement when replacing a geometric proxy, and invalidate evidence when its inputs change. See constraint systems, optimality and verification for the full semantics.

Turn the catalog into an actual run contract

Select only applicable families, then instantiate them with exact targets and values. For each electrical parent, choose a physical construction and any cheap budgets, documenting the implication and assumptions. For example, EVD-01 phase-margin acceptance can generate NET-04 coupled RC budgets, which guide NET-05 width choices; those choices must also satisfy GEO rules and PWR reliability requirements. MAT-03 may be a useful construction for one gradient mechanism while MAT-11 and MAT-12 remain independent obligations.

Review the selected contract for contradictions before search. During search, preserve C invariants and mandatory legality, use B/P evaluators with their declared fidelity, and collect candidate-specific evidence. At completion, resolve every mandatory L/E requirement or report the remaining unknowns. The catalog provides traceability and navigation; the linked chapters provide the derivations, examples, competing effects and validation methods that justify each action.
